TEST REPORT - builder 4 "light" (sky, hills, lights, light layers, cameras, render job) - 21 October
Folder: proc-work/garden/light/

WHAT IS DELIVERED
  stage_light.py     one function  apply(options)  (+ apply_evening(options)); options look 'real' | 'painted'
  render_job.py      draws ONE picture on the graphics card (OptiX, else CUDA), proves the card is used, writes PNG + multi-layer EXR with every light group,
                     and a .json log (engine, device, samples, seed, view transform, exposure, size, camera, seconds, SHA-256 of the scene and of each file)
  regrade.py         after drawing: turn any light group up or down (or recolour it) from the EXR, same finish, about 3 seconds, no card needed
  test_stage_light.py, chain_job.py, rp_round1-5.sh   the tests and the RunPod batches that were run (kept as a record)
  out/               this report, the test JSONs, the logs of the final RunPod pictures (out/logs), small previews (out/previews), two extra sheets
  Pictures for the person (3): in "30 Japanese garden - pictures/": "30 Light - ..." (whole view; veranda and evening; all the way round)

HOW THE FINAL STEP USES IT
  1. optional, FIRST (the weathering kit looks for a camera called "photo"):   stage_light.apply({'steps': ['cameras']})
  2. LAST, after shapes, life, surfaces, weathering:                          stage_light.apply({'look': 'real'})      # or 'painted'
  3. save garden_real.blend / garden_painted.blend, then one picture per run:
       blender -b --disable-autoexec FILE.blend -P render_job.py -- --out /abs/base --camera photo --size 2400x1600 --samples 512 --seed 7 --exr --require-gpu
     (camera 'veranda' for the second view; --exr writes the light-group layers; add --check-groups to get the "groups add up" number)
  4. evening picture: apply_evening({'look':'real'}) after apply(), or give render_job.py  --evening  (it applies it for you)
  5. re-balance after drawing:  blender -b --disable-autoexec -P regrade.py -- base.exr out_base --gain hall=0.5 --gain sun=1.1,1.0,0.9
  Useful options of apply: sun_energy (12), fill_energy (2600), rim_energy (4.5), roof_energy (5000; 0 = no roof lamp), exposure (0.5), vignette (0.55), bloom (0.05).

WHAT WAS NOT TESTED / NOT SEEN
  - No full-size picture (2400 x 1600 at 384-512 samples) was drawn. Estimate from the 960 x 640 numbers: about 40 to 60 s of card time each; not measured.
  - Not tested after the surfaces and weathering stages (they do not exist yet): their materials may change how the sky and the rim light read.
  - Noise of the bright sun glow in the sky at high samples; the OptiX denoiser (only OIDN used).
  - Evening variant was drawn only on the starting file (not on shapes + life): the maple stays bright orange there because its leaf material emits a little light of its own
    (builder 2's material); lowering that emission in the evening is not done by this stage.
  - The pond's white mirror patches of the screens are still there (the pond belongs to builder 2); this stage only made the sky they reflect less bright.
  - Light linking was checked by looking at the rim layer (only maple and roof edge lit), not by a pixel mask.

THINGS I AM UNSURE ABOUT (my judgment, easy to change with the options above)
  - Sun strength 12 instead of 8, fill 2600 instead of 3500: chosen by eye from three pictures (8, 10, 12); the plan did not give a number.
  - Bloom starts at 2.0 (not 1.0) so that only the screens and the lantern glow; sunlit gravel and wall do not bloom.
  - The rim lamp comes from the north-west although the sun is in the south-west: an artistic cheat, allowed by the plan ("back-light the maple"), linked to the maple and roof only.
  - Painted look: sky as flat brush-edged bands with three flat rings round the sun; hills as unequal flat planes (3 to 9 degrees wide, uneven rows), each plane one flat tone
    chosen by how it faces the sun. Light itself (sun, fill, rim, roof lamp, lantern) is the same in both looks, as the plan says.
  - 5.2 note: the Image node must be told layer 'ViewLayer' or it shows no light-group layers (regrade.py does that).
