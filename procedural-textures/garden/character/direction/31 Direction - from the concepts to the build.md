## 0. In one paragraph
Keep the garden exactly as it looks now: same simplified, slightly chunky forms, same dusk colours, same dark value range, same single hot accent (the maple). Then give every material a small, deliberate story that an art director could have drawn on purpose: a bridge that is really built (footing stones, posts, cross beams, stringers, planks across, pegs, a few iron cramps), planks that are no longer boxes (different width and thickness, a little warp, rounded worn edges, chipped corners, end splits, gaps, a few replaced boards), moss and mould only where water and shade put them, roof tiles that differ from each other (a slipped one, a chipped one, moss in the valleys, drip stains under the eaves), grass in clumps of different shapes with seed heads and a few small flowers, a worn bare path, clear shallow water over a pebble bed with a deeper dark middle, ripples, notched lily pads with flowers, iris, and a maple and a pine whose silhouettes follow the concept sheet. Everything is made from real geometry plus real normal maps (the garden has none today). Nothing may look more photographic than the big rocks and the lantern look today.

## 2. "Are there any normal maps used?" (checked on `fix/garden_real_fixed.blend`, 22 October)
No. Facts from opening the scene:
* 0 Normal Map nodes, 0 Displacement nodes. 175 Bump nodes (strength 0.5 to 0.55 on most timber, stone and ground; 1.0 on 48 of them; 0.04 to 0.4 on plants and moss). The scans (`garden/surfaces/maps/*_diff/_disp/_rough_1k.jpg`, 17 sets) give colour, roughness and a height picture that goes into a Bump node. Water and plants get bump from procedural noise.
* None of the 132 mesh objects has a UV map. Every material finds its place on the object from position (box or triplanar mapping inside node groups such as `GARDEN scan old_wood_floor tri-x`). So a normal map in the usual way was not even possible. Giving objects UV maps is therefore part of the job for every builder who adds normal maps (method in section 7).
* The planks (`Astra / varied planks`, 8 runs) are plain boxes with one 3 mm bevel modifier: 224 vertices a run, 1 bevel width, no warp, no thickness change.
The person wants normal maps and real geometry detail now. Both are in every brief.

## 7. Normal maps: how (all builders)
The helper `garden/character/direction/shared/normal_tools.py` is tested (it printed `NORMAL_DIRECTION PASS (right/left under sun from +X: 0.804 vs 0.158; top/bottom under sun from +Y: 0.806 vs 0.156; flipped-green test detects the error: True)` on this computer, Blender 5.2.2, CPU Cycles). Copy it into your own folder and import it.
1. UV first. No object has UVs. For each object that gets a tangent-space normal map write a UV layer named `UVMap` (script, no operators needed): planar top UV `(x, y) / tile_m` for ground and water, or box UV per face (the two coordinates that are not the face's dominant normal axis, divided by tile size in metres) for stones, walls and timber. 1 UV unit = the real size of one tile of your map, so a 2 m wide map tile is `/2.0`. Planks: u along the length, v across, plus a random UV offset per plank so no two planks show the same grain. Roof tiles: put the UV on the prototype mesh `CT / Ceramic pan prototype` (instances inherit it).
2. Make a height picture (white = high): from the scan's `*_disp_1k.jpg`, or procedural (bake a height node setup to an image with an EMIT bake, or draw it with numpy), or by baking from detailed geometry (Cycles bake, type NORMAL, tangent space, default swizzle +X +Y +Z = OpenGL). Keep relief soft and large: blur the height by 1 to 2 pixels at 2048 px, no micro-pores (style note section 0).
3. Convert with `normal_tools.height_to_normal(h, strength=1.5 to 3.0, tile=True)` (OpenGL: green = up the picture = +V). 2048 px for big surfaces (planks, ground, roof, wall), 1024 px for small things. Save with `save_normal_png(path, n)` into `<your folder>/maps/<name>_normal.png` (on the store).
4. In the material: `UV Map -> Mapping (scale to tile) -> Image Texture (Non-Color, Repeat, Cubic) -> Normal Map node (space Tangent, uv_map = UVMap, Strength 0.6 to 1.0) -> Principled Normal`. `normal_tools.add_normal_map_nodes(mat, image, "UVMap", strength, scale)` does exactly this. Remove the old Bump node from the Normal chain on your materials, or keep a Bump only for large lumps (moss) with the Normal Map as its Normal input. Roughness stays from the scan or from your own softly varying map (0.55 to 0.9: matt, painted).
5. GOTCHA found while testing: in Blender 5.2.2 setting `colorspace_settings.name = 'Non-Color'` AFTER writing the pixels of a generated image wipes it to black. Set the colour space right after `images.new` and before writing pixels (the helper does), and do not set it again.
6. TEST LIGHT (required, goes into your report): (a) run `blender -b --factory-startup --python normal_tools.py` once and keep the `NORMAL_DIRECTION PASS ...` line; (b) for each map you ship, render a flat plane with your final material and default UVs under a low sun (30 degrees) from +X and from +Y and check: correlation between the render's fine luminance and `-d(height)/dx` (sun from +X) is at least +0.25, and likewise for +Y with the top of the picture as +V. A map that fails is flipped: fix the map, not the light.
7. Roughness maps may be added the same way; never use more than 4 image maps per material (render time and file size).
8. Real geometry detail is wanted too (planks, posts, roof tiles, tufts, rocks' moss caps, chips): use the normal map for the soft fine part, geometry for what the silhouette and the shadows must show.
