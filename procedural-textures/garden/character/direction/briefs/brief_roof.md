## A. What you build (priority order; steps 1 to 3 together are already a valid delivery)
**Step 0 (10 minutes, do it first): does a UV map on the tile prototype reach all 688 roof tiles?** The roof is geometry nodes (`Hall roof`, modifier `CT / Shared roof controls`, groups `CT / Complete two-bank ceramic roof`, `CT / Roof bank 1 local mechanism`, `CT / Roof bank 2 local mechanism`) that places 688 instances of the hidden 20-vertex mesh `CT / Ceramic pan prototype`. Add a UV layer `UVMap` to that prototype (u along the tile, v across, 1 unit = one tile), put a Normal Map node on a copy of the roof material, draw a close-up with a checker test texture and see whether every tile shows the same UV. If instances do not inherit it, make a copy of the node group, realize the instances inside the copy and store the UV with Store Named Attribute; if that is too slow, use a procedural object-space height for the roof only and say so in the report as the one exception to the normal-map rule.
**Step 1 (about 35 min): roof tile variety and damage, as geometry.** Work in COPIES of the node groups named `G6 roof / ...` and point the modifier at the copy. Per instance: a random `tile_tone` (0 to 1, Store Named Attribute on the instance domain, read in the material with an Attribute node of type Instancer): blue-grey with a little warm and a little green, luminance standard deviation between 0.02 and 0.06; 3 to 6 SLIPPED tiles (down-slope 2 to 5 cm, rotated 2 to 4 degrees); 6 to 12 CHIPPED tiles (swap the instance for one of 3 chipped prototypes `G6 roof / pan chipped a`, `b`, `c`: a missing 4 to 8 cm corner, a crack groove, a broken half); 1 or 2 cracked; 1 or 2 MISSING with a dark underlay patch (`G6 roof / underlay`). Pick the flawed tiles on the south-facing slope so they are visible from the hero camera (-5.2, -5.6, 3.0) and a few near the ridge; put the picked indices in the report.
**Step 2 (about 30 min): roof marks and normal map.** In a copy of `CT / Ceramic painterly surface` (`G6 roof / ceramic`) and of the ridge material: moss as soft olive blobs (#716c36 to #898039 lit, #2a2d24 shaded) in the valleys between the pans, on the last course at the eave and along the ridge, 4 to 10 percent of the visible roof; pale lichen flecks (#c4af8a) 1 to 3 percent; 6 to 10 dark drip stains (#272532 family) hanging from the eave-end tiles; a few leaves in a valley (geometry, 6 to 10, orange-brown). **Step 3 (about 25 min): ridge and eave details.** First look at what the roof already has; then add only what is missing as new objects in `G6 roof` (also link them into `G4 rim receivers` and `G4 roof receivers`): rounded ridge-end caps with a disc relief (`sheet_roof.jpg` top and bottom-left insets), round eave-end discs (14 cm, 3 cm thick, ring relief) on the lowest course (instanced), a rolled ridge course if the ridge is bare. **Step 4 (about 30 min): plaster walls and caps.** Objects `Hall plaster`, `Back wall, left`, `Back wall, right`, `Right wall behind hall` are 8 to 32 vertex boxes: subdivide the visible faces to about 10 cm, cut 2 to 4 plaster-loss patches per long wall (1.5 to 2.5 cm deep, irregular outline, brown clay colour inside), 4 to 8 hairline cracks (single clean dark line, 1 to 2 mm groove), a splash band at the base (darker, 25 cm high), a low stone footing strip (new object, 25 cm). Caps (`... cap` objects): 3 to 6 chipped corners or cracks per cap run, a thin green line of moss along the top edge, lichen specks, drip streaks on the wall below the cap ends. Colour from `sw_plaster.jpg` (soft large blotches; mix 40 percent into the existing plaster colour to stay inside the palette); normal map 2048 x 2048 (soft blotch relief and the cracks), box UV with 1 UV unit = 2.5 m.
**Step 5 (about 35 min): stones.** Rocks `Rock 0` to `4`, pond-edge stones, `Shore stones`, `Water basin` and the basin stones.
