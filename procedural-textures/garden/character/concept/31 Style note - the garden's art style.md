# 31 Style note - the garden's art style (22 October)

What this is: what makes the realistic garden look "stylised, like a game painting" and not like a photograph, written down so that every later step (picture prompts, new geometry, new textures, normal maps) keeps it. The person's rule: "don't make it more realistic. I still want the art style." So the job is to add CHARACTER (wear, moss, construction logic, variety), not realism.

Looked at: real_hero.png and real_veranda.png at full size, plus seven 100 percent crops (roof, bridge, grass, maple, pine and wall, deck, lantern; kept in `crops/`). Numbers below were measured on the two pictures (value = brightness 0 to 1; "detail energy" = average size of the fine contrast left after a 2 pixel blur, on a 0-255 scale, so higher means more fine detail).

## 0. THE RULE FOR EVERY BUILDER (added 22 October on the person's direction)
The person said, verbatim: "Ok. These things need to be integrated into the style. So it's just part of design."

So: wear, mould, moss, cracks, joinery (posts, beams, pegs, cramps), grass tufts, flowers, ripples, stains, chips and every other piece of "character" is a DESIGNED ELEMENT of the garden, drawn in the garden's own visual language: the same simplified, slightly chunky shapes, the same edge treatment (clean, slightly bevelled), the same palette and the same light as the rest of the picture, as if an art director had drawn each one on purpose as part of the style. It is NOT realistic detail laid on top of the picture. Test for any new element: could it sit in a hand-painted game level next to the big rocks and the lantern without looking like it came from a different, more photographic picture? If a detail is finer, sharper, noisier or more "scan-like" than the rocks and the lantern, it fails and must be simplified: fewer, bigger, cleaner shapes (a moss patch is one soft rounded shape with a lighter top edge, a crack is one clean dark line, a peg is one small round shape, a grass tuft is 5 to 9 simple blades).
For builders: this applies to geometry (plank wear is a clean rounded bevel and a few bigger notches, not noise), to colour textures (a few big readable marks, not a dense scan), and to normal maps (soft, large-scale relief: grain grooves, plank edges, tile ridges; no micro-pore noise).

## 1. Form: big simple shapes, chunky, a little soft
* Everything is built from a few big forms. Rocks are blocky, faceted slabs with rounded corners and 4 to 6 readable planes; none has pits, hairline cracks or sharp crystals. Hedges are big dark rounded blobs with no leaves drawn. The pine is stacked flat cloud-discs on one curved trunk. The lantern is stacked slabs (plinth, post, gallery, box, roof) with simple bevels. The hills are flat layered silhouettes. The maple is one large mass made of many same-sized leaf shapes.
* Corners carry a visible, slightly generous bevel (about 1 to 3 cm in real size). Nothing is razor sharp; nothing is worn to mush either.
* Silhouettes read first. If you squint, you can name every object from its outline alone (zigzag bridge, bent pine, hip roof, round-topped maple). Keep that: new character must not break an outline into noise.
* Repeats are allowed and visible (roof tiles in rows, planks in rows, bamboo slats), but each repeat is a clean shape, not a scan.

## 2. Edges and surfaces: clean, calm, no grain
* Edges between objects are clean and fairly crisp; shadow edges are crisp too (low sun). There is no film grain, no camera blur except a gentle depth falloff, no lens dirt, no glints on tiny scratches.
* Surfaces are mostly flat-ish colour with soft large-scale variation (blotches 20 to 60 cm across), plus a few clear marks (a stain, a crack, a patch of moss). They are not covered in pores, hairs or micro-scratches.
* Plaster wall: large soft blotchy stains with a little green at the cap. Roof: blue-grey tiles, a pale yellow lichen fleck here and there. Planks: grey-brown with thin dark grain lines and thin dark joints. Grass ground: mottled olive and brown, a few twigs, a few orange leaves. All of that is low contrast and low frequency.

## 3. Palette: warm key, cool shadows, one hot accent
* Sky and light are peach to rose to dusky violet (the sky near the horizon is about #b58070, the far hills violet-grey, the near hills dark blue-grey). The key light is a low warm orange sun; the shadows are cool blue-violet and green-brown. That warm-against-cool pairing is the picture.
* The hot accent is the maple: saturated red-orange (dark #762103 to bright orange) with a few green leaves mixed in. It is the only strongly saturated large area. Everything else is held back.
* Greens are olive, moss and dusk green (#2a2d24, #423826 in shadow), never fresh spring green. Timber is warm grey-brown (#987a6a, #8e6958). Roof is cool blue-grey. Stone is warm grey-beige with olive moss. Raked sand is cream (#c4ab95 to near white). Shoji paper glows warm cream-white, the lantern window glows warm.
* The 10 most common colours of the hero picture (reduced palette): #2a2d24, #987a6a, #423826, #171711, #8e6958, #695c52, #a78974, #762103, #6e4529, #c4ab95. New detail uses colours from this family: moss (olive to mid green, slightly yellow in light), mould (dark grey-green to near black), iron (rust brown), lichen (pale yellow-green and pale grey), water stain (darker, cooler brown).

## 4. Value range and light
* Brightness percentiles of the hero picture: 1 percent 0.05, 5 percent 0.07, 25 percent 0.18, median 0.32, 75 percent 0.47, 95 percent 0.63, 99 percent 0.88. The veranda picture is similar (median 0.33, 99 percent 0.75). So: a dark picture. Big areas in deep shade (pond, foreground, under the roof), a mid-tone middle, and only a few real brights (shoji screens, lantern window, sand, sky glow). Mean colour saturation about 0.40 in both.
* Light is graded dusk: warm low sun from the side, long cast shadows (pine on the wall, rocks on the grass), a warm rim on the maple, cool fill in the shade, glowing paper screens, a glowing lantern box. Detail must sit inside that light: a plank's moss is darker in shadow and warmer in sun like everything else, never a flat sticker.
* Contrast inside one material stays modest (standard deviation of brightness inside the plank, grass, rock areas is 0.06 to 0.18). New character must not push a surface past that into photographic crunch.

## 5. Texture scale and detail per square metre
* Fine contrast ("detail energy"): roof 8.0, maple 8.4, raked sand 7.5, bridge 5.4, deck 4.0, wall 3.4, grass 3.6, rocks 3.6. Veranda picture: deck 2.8, grass 3.0, lantern 3.7, rocks 3.7. These are LOW numbers. Photographs of the same things sit well above 12.
* Roughly one readable feature per 5 to 10 cm on timber (a grain line, a knot, a joint, a worn corner), per 20 to 30 cm on ground (a tuft, a twig, a moss patch), per 50 cm on stone and wall (a stain, a crack, a moss pad). That is the right size for the new character: bigger and fewer than photographic, but each one meaningful.
* The brief for "character": a viewer should find, anywhere in the picture, one small story per hand-span (a worn corner, a moss rim, a drip stain, a cramp, a peg, a lily flower, a tuft with seed heads), and never a hundred tiny details of equal weight.

## 6. What this style does NOT have (so do not add it)
Photographic noise or grain; hair-level grass blades or individual pores; sharp specular glints on dust; real-looking wet shine except on the pond; depth-of-field blur on the main subject; film bloom beyond the glowing paper and lantern; lens flares; dirt on the lens; people or animals; text; modern objects. And no push towards "photoreal": materials stay slightly painterly, forms stay slightly chunky.

## 7. Short checklist every later step must pass
0. Designed-in test (section 0): every wear, moss, crack, joint, tuft, flower and ripple is drawn in the garden's own simplified, chunky language, not layered-on realism.
1. Squint test: same silhouettes and same big shapes as real_hero.png.
2. Palette test: new colours come from the family in section 3.
3. Detail-energy test: a 100 percent crop of any new area has fine contrast near the numbers in section 5, not above about 10.
4. Light test: new detail is lit by the same dusk light and shadows.
5. One story per hand-span, not noise.

## 8. What the concept images showed (22 October; targets are in "31 Concepts - the targets we work from.jpg")
* Which painter keeps the style: Nano Banana 2 given the rule sentence (section 0) gave the in-style results (hero target concept/gen/hero_A2_posts_nb2.jpg, veranda target concept/gen/veranda_V2_nb2.jpg, all the element sheets). Nano Banana Pro and several Nano Banana 2 prompts without the rule sentence gave photographic moss, hair-fine grass and leafy hedges: they break section 0. Flux 3 stays in style but adds little. Prompts that name "a moss patch is one soft rounded shape, a crack is one clean dark line, a peg is one small round shape, a grass tuft is 5 to 9 simple blades" work.
* Bridge construction to build (concept/gen/sheet_bridge.jpg): short thick posts on flat stone footing blocks standing in the water, two heavy square beams per straight run resting on or notched into the posts, four posts under every square corner landing, deck planks laid across the beams with narrow even gaps, one round wooden peg or a small dark iron cramp per plank end, a clear joint where the two runs meet at the corner, algae band on the posts at the waterline, moss and mould at plank ends and near the water. The posts stop at the deck (the hero target shows a few posts rising above the deck at the edges; that reads as railing posts, which the real bridge does not have, so do not copy that).
* Plank wear to build (concept/gen/sheet_plank2.jpg): a clearly rounded edge and corner on every plank (a real bevel, about 1 to 2 cm), one clean crack with a knot on some planks, round pegs, an iron cramp across two plank ends, a chipped corner, a water-stain band, moss as one soft rounded shape at the ends. The concept planks run paler and more lilac than the garden's grey-brown timber (#987a6a, #8e6958): keep the garden's colours, take only the shapes.
* Roof (sheet_roof.jpg): tiles vary in tone and size, a few chipped, moss as soft olive blobs and lichen as pale shapes in laps and along the ridge, black drip stains below the eave edge, a round end-cap tile. Pond (sheet_pond.jpg): clear water, visible round pebble bed with a few flat stones, algae line at the waterline, calm ripple rings, pink lilies on round pads, iris and reeds. Lantern (sheet_lantern.jpg): worn rounded slabs, soft moss in joints, one or two clean cracks.
* Two known flaws in the targets: the hero target added a moon disc in the sky (not in the garden; ignore it); the tree sheet has a faint ghost of the house at the top.
* Swatches (concept/swatches/*.png): plank wood and plaster are seamless as delivered; grass-and-moss, pond bed and moss were repaired by repainting the centre cross of the half-shifted picture and now tile; the roof tile swatch is weak (too blue, ghost lichen in the blend): use the roof sheet for shapes, not the swatch for colour. All swatches are flatter and more illustrated than the render: use them as colour pictures only after grading toward the garden palette, and never as a replacement for the large soft variation the garden already has.
