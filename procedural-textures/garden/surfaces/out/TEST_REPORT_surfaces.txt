TEST REPORT - builder 3 "surfaces" (materials and weathering), Japanese garden, 21 October
=====================================================================================================
Folder: proc-work/garden/surfaces/
Pictures for the person: ".../proc-work/30 Japanese garden - pictures/30 Surfaces - *.jpg" (three files)

WHAT IT IS, IN PLAIN WORDS
  One stage file, stage_surfaces.py, with apply(options). It changes MATERIALS ONLY (no geometry, lights, world or camera) on the open garden scene,
  in one of two looks:
    look = "real"     photographed surfaces (Poly Haven, free CC0) on everything, then the weathering kit on top
    look = "painted"  the add-on's painted look: broad hand-made colour passages, the add-on's own stone shader retuned, no scans, no kit
  Use:   sys.path.insert(0, '<surfaces folder>');  import stage_surfaces as ss;  ss.apply({'look': 'real'})      (or 'painted')
  Options (all optional): look, weathering (real only, default True), weathering_look (default garden_kept), rebuild_masks ('auto' | 'always' | 'never'),
         maps_dir, pack_images (True before the final save: packs the 62 pictures into the .blend), addon_dir (painted only: also use the add-on's pigment tool), verbose.
  Order in the chain: after shapes and life, before light. It finds objects by name (same patterns as the kit) or by the custom property garden_role, and swaps
  every "GARDEN placeholder <role>" material for its role's material. Glow, leaf, water, hill and sky materials are never touched. Running it twice is safe.
  Files: stage_surfaces.py (the stage), surf_kit.py (node toolbox + scan groups), painted_stack.py, pigment_stage.py (optional), weathering_stage.py (kit part),
         kit/ (private patched copy of the weathering kit), maps/ (16 Poly Haven surfaces, 1k, 24 MB, licence note inside), render_preview.py, render_plates.py, tests/.

  raked gravel                  gravel scan normalised to a pale grey-white (albedo about 0.46); the scan height is only a faint grain, the rake lines stay real geometry.  /  pale
                                grey passages, no grain.
  43 stone materials            Rock Boulder Dry (rocks, piers) or Rock 08 (steps, lantern) by box projection, height as bump only, per-stone tone from the object's random value,
                                Lichen Rock mixed in on steep shaded sides.  /  the add-on's own "Wet Stone" shader kept but retuned: chalky pale fields, short marks, matte.
  roof (3 materials inside the roof nodes)   Grey Roof Tiles colour and roughness, world-position mapping because the tiles are instances; mean raised from 44 to 64 (of 255).
                                /  the add-on's painted ceramic shader lifted out of crushed black.
  veranda boards (runs 0-2), bridge boards (runs 3-7)   Weathered Planks (amber-brown) and Wood Planks Grey (silver); each of the 56 tone materials has its own tint and its own
                                picture offset; boards run along x (measured, see below).  /  tone kept, silver on the bridge, interrupted wear streaks along each board.
  hall posts, beams, shoji bars Old Wood Floor, grain up the posts and along the beams.  /  board-directed flat grain with interrupted wear (or the add-on's pigment tool, optional).
  hall plaster                  White Stucco dulled to grey-cream, soft macro variation.  /  held and lost edges, drip tails running up the wall.
  3 garden walls (own material now)   Worn Plaster Wall with Worn Cracked Plaster at the foot.  /  earth-cream passages.

  Weathering kit (real only)    dust-and-algae film, runoff under caps and eaves, damp foot, plaster hairlines and stone chips, wall growth, dirt, patch moss.  Not used: steps,

WHAT I DID NOT TEST OR AM UNSURE OF
  * Not tested after builder 4's world, lights and hills, nor on builder 2's test files (only builder 1's), nor in EEVEE. Colour balance depends on the final light (the pine bark and the
    stones look warmer in the orange sun than in the plates).
  * Not seen at 100 percent: scans are 1k. Close crops of ground and gravel may be soft.
  * Kit dials are first guesses. The kit draws moss in flat painted tones (built for the film's painter), so it looks like paint on a photo. Found by a layer-by-layer sweep: the pale-mint blotches
    on stones came from patch moss (groundmoss), not from the wall-growth moss dial I tuned first. Now stones are grey-beige with a few small green patches and small pale lichen spots
    (they read a little like polka dots). Left for the final step to tune by eye.
  * Roof: scan tile rows do not line up with the add-on's tile rows (world-position mapping); it reads as aged tile with yellow-green moss on crests, not proven at hero distance in a final.
  * Ground uses a from-above projection (it is a gentle height field) instead of box projection; steep sides of the mound could stretch a little. Not seen.
  * Painted look: judged by eye only; passages are my reading of the add-on's rules. The add-on's pigment tool works on Blender 5.2.2 (4 objects, 0.1 s each) but swaps each object's
    mesh for an identical private copy and adds a hidden handle object per target; the difference to my hand-made passages is small, so it is OFF by default (addon_dir turns it on, tested once).
  * Switching looks in one session: painted after real works (stones fall back to hand-made nodes), real after painted works. Best: reopen the start file.
  * "Wet means darker, greener, glossier" not tried (state kept dry).
