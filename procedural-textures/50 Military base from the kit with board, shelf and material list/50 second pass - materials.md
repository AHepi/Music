# Materials: a British army camp at dusk after rain, 1915 (dressed second pass)

Started from the first builder's list (work/materials.md). Everything the first builder listed is kept as it was. What this pass adds is marked ADDED; a line the pass changed is marked CHANGED.

Who is here (not in the picture): a gun battery's men and the camp guard, billeted in bell tents along a duckboard track to a timber watch tower.
What they do all day: drill, haul shells and boxes to the gun, stand watch on the tower, dig, mend wire, cook, wait out the rain.
What they left behind when they stepped out of frame: the first builder's lantern, bucket, mess tin, gun, shells, sandbag wall with spade, wire coil and stakes, drums and crates, telegraph line. This pass adds: a tin mug, a tobacco tin and a soft cap on the lantern crate (the man who sat there for his tea); a pair of muddy boots at the foot of that crate (taken off, left to drain); a wire mallet lying by the wire coil (the wire party put it down mid-job); a pick leaning on the sandbag wall beside the spade (the digging party); a tarpaulin tied over the stacked ammunition boxes and a canvas cover tied on the gun's muzzle (the gun crew keeping the rain off); a rammer staff leaning on the gun shield; a rain barrel at the guardroom corner under the roof edge (the cook's water); spare duckboard lengths stacked beside the track (the track party); an open ammunition crate with shell noses showing, beside the lamp box (the gun crew mid-job).

## Traps

- A lantern with its light inside its glass lights nothing; the light sits just outside the chimney.
- The tall frame is narrow near the camera; check what the camera sees before dressing.
- Wet ground gone pale or black: judge it beside the brightest lamp.
- Dressing that crosses the frame's lines (a rope across the gun, a crate in front of the door) is wrong however good it looks; nothing added crosses the track, the tower or the gun's barrel.
- Wear on every edge looks like a filter: the edge rubbing is faint and only on timber, iron and the gun.
