# Judges, round 2: pictures A, B and C

Three more fresh judges (Opus), each given only the judging folder, now with a third picture: C is the control builder's own scene with the look stage dropped onto it and a dial pass set from the measures (540 x 960; the sheet told them to judge what it shows, not its size). Key, hidden from them: A = the control build (Blender alone), B = the plugin build, C = the control scene with the look stage. The first three judges could not be asked again (their transcripts could not be reopened), so these are three new judges scoring all three pictures. Their answers, unedited.

Choice: B. It is the only one that already belongs with the reference pictures: a dark blue night where a little warm light from the windows, the porch lamp and the fire carries the whole picture, with haze in the air and worn boards on the house. Its worst fault, the toy-like things in the front, sits in one patch that can be swapped out, while A and C would need their light rebuilt from scratch. It still needs the sea to show and one clear sign of Samoa today, such as an open-sided fale nearby or a solar panel on the roof.
Fix A: Make it dusk instead of bright day, so the sky turns deep blue and the only warm light comes from the house's lit windows and porch lamps.
Fix B: Take out the toy-like things in the front (the white spiky fire, the black star of sticks and the ring) and put in a small real driftwood fire with blackened stones, or leave the sand bare.
Fix C: Switch on lamps inside the house so the windows glow and the porch lights light the deck, and turn down the orange glow on the sand that comes from nowhere.

## Judge 5

| Picture | Photograph | Request | Film backdrop in the look |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 4 | 7 | 2 |
| B | 2 | 5 | 5 |
| C | 3 | 6 | 3 |

Choice: B. It is the only one whose light, colour and air already belong with the reference pictures: a dark blue night with haze among the palms, and warm light from the windows, the porch lamp and the fire that really falls on the walls and the sand. A is a bright, pastel daytime picture far outside the look, and C, though dark, lights its beach orange from nowhere and has no haze, so both would need their light redone. B's faults sit in a few objects at the front of the picture, and its sea and its signs of today need to show more clearly, but all of that can be changed without touching the light that makes it belong.
Fix A: Move the time to after sunset: let the sky and the sea go dark blue, and switch on warm lamps in the windows and on the porch so their light falls on the deck, the sand and the canoe.
Fix B: Take out the black star of sticks, the nested black rings and the spiky white "crystal" fire at the front of the picture, and put a real driftwood fire with soft flames and a little smoke in their place.
Fix C: Show where the warm light comes from, such as a lamp on the porch or a fire on the sand, and let it light only the ground near it, so the rest of the beach falls into blue shadow instead of glowing orange all over.

## Judge 6

| Picture | Photograph | Request | Film backdrop in the look |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 4 | 7 | 1 |
| B | 2 | 5 | 5 |
| C | 3 | 6 | 3 |

Choice: B. B is the only one that already has the series' light: a dark blue night where the little warm light there is does something (glowing louvres, a pool from the porch lamp, firelight on the sand and the smoke), with fog putting air between the house and the palms. Its weathered boards and rust-edged iron roof make the bach look old and used, while A is bright pastel daylight with clean new paint and C keeps A's clean, toy-bright props under a warm glow that comes from nowhere. What is wrong with B sits in the props along the bottom edge, which can be swapped out without touching the house, the light or the air that already work.
Fix A: Turn the bright pastel daylight into a dark blue dusk in which the only warm light comes from the bach's own lamps and windows.
Fix B: Replace the spiky glowing fire, the black starburst, the ring and the blocky stones along the bottom with a small real-looking driftwood fire and a few ordinary beach stones, or clear them away.
Fix C: Give the warm light a source you can see, such as lit windows and a porch lamp, and let the beach go dark where that light cannot reach instead of the whole foreground glowing orange from nowhere.
