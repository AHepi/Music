**Verdict: nearly.** The colours, the framing and the finish already match the references. The light on the bottom third of the frame doesn't, and neither do most of the objects sitting there.

**Fixes, most visible first**

1. **DIAL.** What a viewer sees: the sand across the whole bottom third of the frame is lit pale cream, close to white around the fire. That makes the bottom-left corner the brightest part of the picture, not the hut. What to change: the fire's own light should warm only a small amber pool of sand around it, and the rest of the sand should fall into blue-grey shadow, so the hut's lit windows and porch carry the picture.

2. **SHAPE.** What a viewer sees: at bottom left the flames are a crown of white crystal spikes standing in a ring of white, sharp-cornered stones, and the whole fire glares pure white. What to change: a low, ragged fire of amber and orange with glowing embers, in rounded stones blackened by soot, with a thin trail of smoke rising from it and catching its light.

3. **SHAPE.** What a viewer sees: along the bottom edge there is a perfect fan of thin black rods (bottom left), and a ring with flat cut sides and stepped insides next to a straight pale beam with cut sides (bottom middle). They read as geometric shapes, not as things anyone would leave on a beach. What to change: worn things a viewer can name at a glance, with ragged ends, such as a heap of driftwood or firewood, a coil of frayed rope and a split, half-burnt log.

4. **SURFACE.** What a viewer sees: the plank path, porch steps, chairs and door are pale, even and unmarked like new timber, and the sand is smooth and untrodden. What to change: grey, salt-bleached, split boards with some sunk into the sand or missing, scuffed chairs, peeling paint on the door, and footprints, ash, seaweed and damp patches in the sand.

5. **SHAPE.** What a viewer sees: behind the porch on the right, a big smooth grey mass about as tall as the porch has no edges or detail, like a giant bean standing on end. What to change: give it an outline a viewer can name, such as the ribs and planks of an upturned boat or the cracks and ledges of a rock, or take it out so the dark sea shows there.

6. **SHAPE.** What a viewer sees: the round globe lamp over the door throws a hard-edged, near-white wedge of light down the wall and door, like a modern electric porch light. What to change: an old oil lantern hanging from the porch beam, with an amber glow that fades softly across the wall and door.

**What already works**

The deep teal-blue sky, the palms as dark, slightly hazy shapes against it, and the warm glow of the louvred windows are the references' two colour families; keep them. The framing works: tall frame, the hut centred as the one hero, the eye at standing height, and the plank path leading in from the bottom right the way the rails do in the pithead painting and the coal-mine render. Scale comes from the chairs, steps, door and boat, with no people. The soft painted finish matches the coal-mine render's.

**Where the measures and my eyes disagree**

- **Brightness:** the measures say "inside" the band. That is only because the dark top two-thirds pull the average down. The bottom third is far brighter than the bottom of any reference picture. Trust the eyes and do fix 1.
- **Warm light:** the measures say "high" for both warm share and warm light coverage (coverage is about four times the top of the band), and my eyes agree. But the measures' advice blames the windows. The windows are only a small part of it; most of the extra warm light is the fire's light on the sand. Turning down only the window light or the exposure would darken the hut along with everything else and leave the bright patch in the bottom-left corner.
