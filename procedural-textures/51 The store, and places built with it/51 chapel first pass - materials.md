# Materials: a chapel at dusk (a British Nonconformist chapel, about 1900, in a wet mill-town lane)

Who is here (not in the picture): the chapel keeper, an old man who lights the lamps and sweeps the steps, and the small congregation arriving for the evening service.
What they do all day: he sweeps, trims the oil lamps, stokes the stove, wipes the benches; the congregation scrape their boots, hang wet umbrellas, sing.
What they left behind when they stepped out of frame: a besom broom leant by the door, a zinc bucket on the step, a lit hurricane lantern on the top step, a boot scraper at the foot of the steps, a door left ajar with lamplight spilling out, a wet umbrella propped against the railing, a milk can by the gate.

## Changes kept true during the build (the list follows the picture)
- Gate piers: gritstone (SURFACE old_stone_wall_02, darkened) with dressed caps and pyramid tops; damp at the foot and soot streaks made as stain planes. Gate leaves swung open inwards.
- Front railing: wrought iron bars with spear tops on a very low coping, so the graveyard shows through it.
- Lane gutter: mud stain over the setts along the railing foot (made in code, dark brown, glossy).
- Headstones: pale and slate stone slabs with round tops, leaning (no words); grass tufts (store weeds) in the yard.
- Far terraces: dark brick fronts with slate roofs and stacks, a few lit windows, lost in the haze (made in code, SURFACE dark_brick_wall); a mill chimney and sheds far behind.
- Second gas lamp (store part) in the yard on the right; a hurricane lantern hung on an iron bracket by the door (store part).
- Window glass: amber glow made in code (one front window and the vestry window lit; the other front window left dark).
- Downpipes at both front corners: cast iron with rust at the brackets; rust runs under them.
- Notice board by the door: dark frame with blank aged paper.
- Fallen leaves on the flags and the setts; a drain grating in the gutter; the lane's puddles sit where the lamps and the door glow would mirror.
- Roof slopes: slate courses made in code (brick-pattern, wet sheen), instead of the store slate.

## Traps (operational)
- The wall box is solid: anything placed behind the wall face (y beyond 11.5 at the front) is hidden; door glow, glass and frames sit in front of it.
- The camera is 6 m back from the gate so the railing and yard show; nearer, the piers fill the frame and hide the yard.
- Stain planes are see-through shaders: keep them off shadow casting (done) and in front of the stone relief, not inside it.
