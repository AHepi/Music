# Materials: what I added to the chapel lane (dressing pass, 4 November 2026)

Who is here (not in the picture): the chapel keeper (an old man) who stokes the stove, rakes out the ash and sweeps the steps, the congregation hurrying in out of the rain, and the milkman and the coalman's horse who came earlier.
What they do all day: keep the stove in, throw ash on the wet ground so boots do not slip, carry milk for the tea urn, lay two boards over the gutter mud so the congregation can reach the flags dry-shod.
What they left behind when they stepped out of frame: a wheelbarrow of stove ash with its shovel and a spill of ash, the keeper's lit lantern and coal scuttle set down on the setts while he works, a milk churn by the gate, two boards over the gutter mud, a wet umbrella and a hymn book dropped on the setts, horse droppings and straw, loose coal.

Changes kept true during the build (the list follows the picture)
- Sacks: first planned, built, and taken out; they read as stones. Replaced by the coal scuttle and the lantern.
- Boot-mud and cinder heaps drawn as solid ragged patches read as brown paper; replaced by see-through smears.
- Long wet-dark rut strips across the setts were drawn and removed: they muddied the puddles.
- Flag-path wear smears made the glossy path dull; the pale worn-flag smears were removed and the rest made thin and glossy.
- Churn moved from the right edge to by the pier: it stood on the reflection path of the right puddle and blacked it out.
- Added: the keeper's lit lantern (its own light), the hymn book, headstone stains.

## Traps (operational)
- Anything standing between a puddle and the lamp or the door glow blacks out that puddle's reflection; keep tall things off those lines (the right puddle and the second gas lamp).
- The flags' tops are 0.07 to 0.09 m, the setts' tops about 0 (plus or minus 0.01): smears on the flags sit at 0.1, smears on the setts at 0.006.
- Soft rough smears on the glossy flags or over a puddle kill the shine; give them low roughness and low strength or leave those places alone.
- Camera pixel positions must be worked out at the final 540 x 960, not the default render size, or everything lands in the wrong place.
- In the 9:16 frame the bottom edge is only 2 m wide and about 3 m in front of the gate: things placed at the gutter (y 0 to 2) sit in the top third of the lower half and come out small.
