Painter brief: old (dry street) and new (wet street)

Old = the notice corner painting's brief (the lockout painting's own brief was not saved). Red = removed, green = added.

OLD

Repaint image 1 completely as a hand-painted illustration in the exact painting style of image 2: the same visible brush strokes, dark ink outlines, simplified painterly textures on brick, stone, slate and wood, and matte painted finish. Nothing should look like a 3D render or a photograph any more. It is a pit-village street at dusk on 1 May 1926. Keep image 1's camera, framing, perspective and every shape, outline, size and position exactly as they are, and keep its pattern of light and dark; change only the brushwork and the colour handling. Keep: the rusty drum with its open top, dull dark rust-brown, not orange, its top a dark sooty hollow with only a faint orange glow low inside behind the front rim; the empty old wooden kitchen chair with worn, grubby pale paint, darker than the notice, its back to the board, with a small hurricane lantern on its seat (amber glass, a small flame inside); the wooden pail at the chair's feet, with no handle; the dark timber notice board on its two posts with the cream notice (plain paper with grey rows of print-like marks, no readable letters); the black gas lamp standing free on its own post above the board; the brick terraces, the colliery building, its tall chimney and winding wheels; and far up on the left the small two-wheeled handcart seen from behind, the coal sacks and the dustbins. The colliery gates are shut: thin dark iron bars across a dim grey doorway whose big doors are closed. Every gas lamp has warm amber glass with a small bright gas mantle inside. The gutter beside the kerb is a shallow, smooth stone channel with no steps, ridges or grating. The road, pavement and walls are cool blue-grey dusk, and the brick is weathered and greyed by coal smoke without getting any darker. Warm light stays only where it already is: round the drum, on the board, on the chair seat, at the lamps and in the few lit windows. Nothing is brighter than the notice. Thin parts (the lantern's wire handle, the lamp arms, the gate bars, the chair rungs, the winding rope) stay thin and exactly in place, or are left out; never thicken them. Where two things meet or nearly meet, keep each edge exactly where it is and never join them: the drum's side and the board's left post, the gas-lamp head and the wall pipe and bracket beside it, the far lamps and the house downpipes, the chair back across the bottom of the notice; the winding rope never joins the lamp. Never add: flames, sparks, smoke or steam anywhere (not from the drum, not from any chimney); glows, halos or light rays round any light, the notice or the drum; a handle on the pail; people, animals or birds; extra props (no coal tub, rails, barrel, crates or litter in the road or on the pavement); puddles or reflections; a moon, stars or bright clouds; new signs or lettering; new or longer shadows.
