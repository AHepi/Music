===== shot-planner
--- summary
I built the shot planner (kit/calculators/plan_shot.py). I also taught the plugin kit's shot checker (paper-film/tools/check_shot.py) to read a check file's "accepted" list and its kinds of shot. Both test files pass: 60 of 60 checks for the planner and 20 of 20 for the checker. I spent nothing, did not change the film kit, the round 5 Works files or the checkout files, and did not commit.

What the planner does, in plain words:
- **Input.** It takes a place's facts and a short shot list: the subject, the story things and the kind of shot.
- **Planning.** It places the camera:
  - across the street, in line with the near edge of the subject or its support (rule 1);
  - at a standing eye height above the surface under it (rule 2);
  - as far back as the nearest story thing still reaches 0.72 down the frame (rule 7's 0.70 plus a margin).
- **Turn, slide and plate.**
  - It turns the camera so the street's lines meet where wanted, then slides it sideways, never turning, to clear the phone buttons (rule 12).
  - For an overview it raises the eye and never tilts.
  - It starts the slide back toward the subject's middle and keeps it level.
  - It works out the longest slide the painting allows at the nearest painted thing, the plate lens needed (from where the frame's corners land along the slide) and the plate rows needed.
- **People and props.** It can propose a loose crowd, two deep across the view. It can also give the turn that points a prop's front at a target.
- **Judging.** It judges any camera: as planned, as built, or at another spot. It predicts what the checker will measure:
  - where the lines meet and how far that is from the subject, the eye height, the reach down the frame, and the right edges near the phone buttons;
  - the story box, the main rule's spots, the subject's size, the work props and the bottom-left quarter;
  - the people: backs turned and at least 6 m from every camera place along the slide; a crowd standing at least 60 degrees across every view; two rows deep; shoulders 0.3 to 0.5 m apart; heads at least a head width apart on screen;
  - which way the signs face.
  Each failure says by how much, in metres.
- **The skeptic's changes, followed:**
  - The limits come from the view's kind of shot.
  - Exceptions in the place's accepted list come out as "known", unless they have grown more than 5% past what was accepted.
  - There is no silhouette fallback. A face that shows gets the painter's "face lost in shadow" words and a skin check after painting.
  - Rule-4 near-touches are not predicted. Box estimates that go past a limit say "look" (confirm on the render), not "fail".

What the checker change does:
- With "kind_of_shot", a problem that kind of shot switches off or loosens moves to a new "Known and allowed" section, and the kind's replacement test is added. For a crowd that is heads apart and every figure half seen, for the groups listed under "figures". For a dark subject it is the subject's outline against the light round it.
- With "accepted", the problems the project chose to keep come out as known.
- With neither key, the report is word for word what it was before. The only change is the line that names the checker's version, which follows the file's contents. The marked picture is byte for byte the same.

--- api
**plan_shot.py** (kit/calculators; Python 3, standard library and the core):
- **Command line.**
  - `plan_shot.py --place-facts F --shots LIST [--shot NAME ...]`
  - `plan_shot.py --place-facts F --view V [--plan] [--at x,y,z --turn D --plate-at x,y,z]`
  - Options: --count-ground; --strict (exit 1 on any failure that is not accepted, for a preflight); --json, --out, --report, --settings, as every calculator.
- **Shot list keys** (all documented at the top of the file): name, view, kind_of_shot, subject, support, story (with "group:NAME" for every figure of a group), work, fill, must_read, box_excludes, crowds, things, figures, crowd_layout, turn_toward, and camera.
  - things: {name: {base or centre, size}}. figures: replace or add figures.
  - crowd_layout: {group, count, front_middle}. turn_toward: {prop: {target, front_axis_degrees}}.
  - camera: plan, at, turn_degrees, plate_at, stands_on, eye_height_metres, line_up (near edge, centre or far edge), meeting_point_across (a number, straight or rightmost), spot (an overview's window), along, slide_metres.
- **Output, for each shot:**
  - camera: composed, plate, turn_degrees, eye_above_floor_metres, plus lined_up_across_metres and buttons_slide_metres when planned;
  - meeting_point;
  - slide: length, limit, nearest_painted_thing, magnification, longest_slide_metres, plate_lens_needed_degrees, plate_rows_needed, turn_allowance_degrees, sideways_allowance_metres;
  - steps (plain words), checks, closed_forms, proposed_figures and prop_turns when asked, counts, verdict (fail, look or pass) and assumptions.
  - Each check is {rule, about, verdict (pass, fail, look, known, info), measured, limit, fix, estimate, things}, plus extras such as nearer_metres, sideways_metres and rows.
  - The top level also carries settings_used.
- **From Python:** Place(facts, settings, shot_spec), Frame(settings, facts), Shot(name, spec, place, settings, frame, view, count_ground).run(), plus helpers: long_axis, footprint_gap, worse_than_accepted, hit_floor, ray, solve_along, solve_height and solve_turn.

**check_shot.py** (plugin kit only):
- New check-file keys, documented in its note: "kind_of_shot", "accepted" (each entry {rule, things?, reason, who, date, measured?}; the rule is a profile rule name or the report's rule words), "figures", "fill" and "support_surfaces".
- New option --settings (default: kit/film-settings.json, found through kit/calculators/settings.py, which is read only when one of the new keys is present).
- With a new key: the report gains a "Known and allowed" section and the header counts the known problems, and known.json is written: {kinds, settings_file, settings_used, problems, known}.
- Report.add takes rule_id, measured and things; nothing printed changes.
- apply_kinds_and_accepted(report, shot, settings_path) returns None and does nothing when the check file has neither key.
- check-shot.sh needs no change.

===== views-and-models
--- summary
I built three tools, and all three are tested on the project's own recorded cases. The whole calculator test suite passes: 11 test files, 0 failed. I spent nothing, committed nothing and changed no film kit, round 5 or checkout file.

1. **view_audit.py** (kit/calculators) works from a place facts file. It checks each view for:
- **Letter height in pixels.** Height comes from the up-and-down part of the view and width from the across part, as the skeptic asked. A board turned 60 degrees keeps its letter height; one tipped back 60 degrees halves it. Words the page needs fail under 20 px and warn at 20 to 25 px, and the tool gives the letter size that would pass.
- **Drum turns** that face every camera reading them.
- **Loud signs the page never names.** Loudness is letter pixels times letter brightness, measured against the least legible named sign that still reads. The tool says how far to dim.
- **Backs of signs that show, and chimney tops** in or just under the band kept for typed words.
- **Figures:** head size, whether a face can show, and how near each figure stands.
- **Continuity counts** of figures per view and state.
- **Title contrast:** the dark band a title needs on a still.

2. **make_check_file.py** (kit/paper-film/blender) runs inside Blender after a place's assemble script. Only the story choices come from a short shot list: subject, support, story, work, and any groups the author merges, splits or adds. Everything else is measured from the scene:
- groups, one per prop and one per figure, with loose parts joined to the prop they sit in
- must_read, from lettering materials
- background pairs
- ground and resting, from rays down; indoors only on a declared floor, never on a counter
- light props, which are story groups whose own surface glows over 15%
- eye range, rule 7 and sky pattern, from the view's kind of shot
- the title zone, or "band": true where the words go over a dark band
- the keys the plugin's check_shot now reads: kind_of_shot, figures, support_surfaces and accepted

It rebuilt every hand-made group of the notice corner (15), W1 (17), W3 (18) and checkout C1 (14), with the same story roles. It also found facts the hand files missed:
- the lantern stands on DIESEL's lid
- the lunch tin stands on PETROL's lid
- W3's drums belong in must_read; the round 4 critic read "DIESE" there.

3. **model_lint.py** (kit/paper-film/blender) checks .glb files with plain Python, or the open Blender scene. It finds:
- stripes, using round 3's find_stripes thresholds
- two surfaces on the same plane
- floors laid just over another ground
- '.001' names
- glass reading its see-through value from a picture
- glowing materials with light base colours
- index sizes against the saved model
- each model's front direction

**Findings for the lead:**
- **W4 shows 6 men, not 8.** In round 5's W4, two of No. 2's eight men (men 4 and 8) stand wholly outside the frame at the composed spot. Blender's own boxes in works-shot.json agree. The plan's count is 8.

--- api
**view_audit.py** (kit/calculators; plain Python)
- Command line: `python3 view_audit.py audit --place-facts F [--view V ...] [--named "a,b"] [--timeline T --page N] [--levels levels.json] [--figure-states "group=flag,..."] [--count-groups "g1,g2"] [--expect-counts "w3/quiet=7,..."]`.
- Command line: `python3 view_audit.py title-contrast --still P --box l,t,r,b --text-colour ffffff`.
- Both commands take --json, --out, --report and --settings. Exit 1 when a needed word fails or a needed sign shows its back.
- The --levels file looks like {view: {sign: lit levels | {"filmed": n}}}.
- Python functions: `audit(facts, settings, views, named, narration_words, levels, figure_states, count_groups, expect_counts)` returns views, stencil_turns, loud_signs, landmarks, figures, continuity, problems, warnings and notes. Also `letter_measure(sign, camera)`, `needed_height`, `figure_box`, `title_contrast`, `narration_of_page`.
- Signs may carry an optional "letters_up" vector, for a sign lying flat.

**make_check_file.py** (kit/paper-film/blender; runs inside Blender, and answers --help outside it)
- Use: `blender -b BASE.blend --python ASSEMBLE.py --python make_check_file.py -- --shot-list SHOT.json --check-file OUT.json [--place-facts F] [--compare HAND.json] [--out RESULT.json] [--report R]`. For a saved scene: `blender -b SCENE.blend --disable-autoexec --python make_check_file.py -- ...`.
- Shot list keys: view, kind_of_shot, subject, support, story, work, groups (patterns), names (display names; two names may share one), prop_collections, set_objects, fill, support_surfaces, lines, sky, accepted.
- The check file it writes has: size, groups, subject, support, story, lights_in_story, pairs_also, work, on_ground, stands_on, must_read, box_excludes, lines, lines_key, eye_height, title_zone, band, sky, story_reaches_down, kind_of_shot, figures, fill, support_surfaces, match_excludes, own_surface_materials and accepted.
- The result JSON adds the measured facts and, with --compare, the comparison against the hand-made file.

**model_lint.py** (kit/paper-film/blender)
- Use: `python3 model_lint.py FILES.glb [--as-set] [--index index.json] [--write-index NEW.json] [--only stripes,same-plane,floors,names,glass,glow,sizes,front] [--prop]`.
- Inside Blender it checks the open scene: `blender -b X.blend --disable-autoexec --python model_lint.py -- ...`. Add --lint-files to check .glb files instead.
- It takes --json, --out, --report and --settings. Exit 1 on stripes, same-plane faces, floors over ground, glass alpha from a picture, or a prop file's copy-numbered root.
- Python functions: `parts_from_glb(path)`, `parts_from_blender()`, `lint(models, settings, only, as_set, index, one_prop)`, `write_index`.

**For the preflight:** each tool's JSON has problems/warnings or failed lists. make_check_file's comparison lists any hand group whose objects differ, per role.

What each tool does:

- paint_request.py (before paying for a painting)
  - `preflight` checks the plate and stops on a failure. It looks for:
    - render faults in the log, and light problems in the shot checker's report;
    - thin parts the painter will smear, and lettering it will garble;
    - even bright discs, and black holes in the set (log 32's open-looking doorway);
    - light inside the top of a hollow prop (lesson 138);
    - heads paler than coats;
    - coat colours the film's finish turns red (I copied the stage's colour finish from cinema-stage.js into Python);
    - figures barely darker than what is around them;
    - figures casting long shadows (lesson 135);
    - real people's names.
  - `prompt` writes the main prompt and the "behind" prompt from two template files. The tool measures which things to name, their order, where warm light stays, thin parts, near pairs, the people (how many, near or far, which way they face), whether to say "no faces", and what the behind painting must remove. The words describing each thing come from a person: a `paint_as` field in the check file and a short brief. It adds the skeptic's clauses: far walkers are flat dark shapes with no pale spots; a facing man's face is lost in shadow with no lit cheek; the figures stay as dark as in the plate.
  - `price` multiplies the dated rate by the megapixels returned plus every picture sent, and marks the result as an estimate. `fit-price` fits the rate once real charges are read.
  - `request` runs all of it and writes the request file. It never sends anything. It refuses if the preflight fails, if there is no render log or shot report, or if the price would go past the spending line.
- after_painting.py (when a painting comes back)
  - Resizes the painting to the plate. I found the ledgers used Lanczos resizing; with it the people test's resized painting matches exactly.
  - Always lines it up, using the skeptic's pass rules.
  - Checks each figure's outline against that figure's own plate baseline, looks for person-sized dark shapes the plate does not have, checks each figure's darkness against its surroundings in plate and painting, and counts skin-coloured pixels on each head.
  - Compares the style with the standard painting (warnings only).
  - Lines up the behind painting and keeps the painted shadows round near props, checking the ground beside them. It also checks that people who leave take their shadows with them.
  - Writes PNG only.
- plan_states.py (held shots that change on a spoken word)
  - Lists every plate needed, including a behind plate for a slide past people.
  - Errors: a change during a slide; two changes on different words sharing one plate. Warnings: only people change (lesson 136); a cue in the words band; busy states, with workers' head sizes.
  - With `--measure` it runs make_state_picture.py on the plates. Up to 0.12 of the frame is a cut; above that it is a 0.8 s blend in 3 steps. It also gives the long-shadow ratio, thresholds set from the measured noise, and the exact commands.

--- api
Command line (each command answers --help, prints a plain report or --json, accepts --out, --report and --settings, and names the settings it used):

- paint_request.py
  - `preflight --check C --facts PLATE_FACTS --plate P [--helper-plate H] [--render-log L] [--shot-report R] [--real-names F] [--place-facts PF --view V] [--exposure X] [--hollow G...] [--allow-long-shadows] [--work-folder D]`. Exits 1 on a failure.
  - `prompt` takes the same options plus `--brief B` (required) and `[--behind-remove figures|G...]`.
  - `price [--returned WxH] [--send WxH...] [--behind] [--rate-cents R] [--spent-dollars D]`.
  - `request` takes all of the above plus `[--style FILE] [--out-folder D] [--ledger L] [--ignore-preflight]`. It writes paint-request.json, paint-prompt.txt, behind-prompt.txt and near-list.json, and adds a "planned" ledger entry. It never sends.
  - `fit-price --charges FILE`, where FILE is a list of {request_id, cents, output "WxH", inputs ["WxH"...]}.
- after_painting.py
  - `check --painting RETURNED --plate P --check C --facts PLATE_FACTS --out-folder D [--behind RETURNED_BEHIND] [--near G... | --near-list near-list.json] [--helper-plate H] [--figures G...] [--standard S]`. Writes <name>-plate-size.png, <name>-aligned.png and, for a behind painting, -aligned.png and -shadows-kept.png. Exits 1 on a failure.
  - `style --painting P [--standard S]`.
  - `figures --painting LINED_UP --plate P --check C --facts F`.
- plan_states.py
  - `plan --plan PLAN.json [--measure] [--plate-folder D] [--facts-folder VIEW=D...] [--work-folder D] [--out-folder D]`. Exits 1 on an error; writes state-plan.json.
  - The plan file holds place_facts, plates {"view/state": file}, field_groups {field: [check groups]}, people_fields {place-facts figure group: state field}, an optional states table (for places without one), and pages [{page, view, start_state, slides [{from, to}], behind_state?, changes [{word, time, to_state, changes [fields], cue_points?, cue_radius_metres?, outlines?}]}].

Inputs that new places supply:
- The check file gains `paint_as` {group: words; "" means another group's words cover it}, `behind_as`, `hollow` [groups] and `figures` (which check_shot already reads).
- A painting brief JSON: place_sentence, set_keep, notes, lamps_sentence, colour_sentence, warm_words {group or "set"}, thin_words, thin_also, pair_words {"a|b"}, extra_pairs, removed_parts, library_props, never_add_notes, people {dress, near_noun, near_what, behind_note, far_doing, near_where, remove_note}, and behind {remove, behind_words, warm_words, keep_words}.

Python API in painting_measures.py:
- Loading and masks: load_shot(check, facts), read_rgb, read_painting_at_plate_size, mask_at(mask, size, smooth).
- Figures: figure_groups(shot, place_facts), figure_record, figure_surround_ratio, skin_pixels, head_region, head_and_coat.
- Colour: stage_finish(rgb, look), hue_degrees_and_saturation, in_hue_band.
- Running the kit's tools: run_tool, align(...) (returns before, after, stretch, shift), state_mask_shares(...) (returns mask, outline and full shares, noise, levels outside).
- Saving: is_lossless.

All the sums come from frame_geometry (projection, sizes, parallax, reach_distance), settings (values, resolve_profile) and the kit's own align_painting.py, keep_painted_shadows.py, make_state_picture.py and check_shot.py; none are copied. The other tools call these through subprocess or import.
