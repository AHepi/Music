P1 So... won't creating money just cause inflation? Inflation means prices, in general, going up over time. And in last time's story, they did. In Britain, everyday prices roughly doubled during the war... and by 1920 they were about two and a half times what they'd been in 1914. So was it the new money? Back to the shell factory.
P2 remember... a country can run out of workers, materials and machines. in the war, it did. fewer than one in a hundred were out of work. submarines sank nearly eight million tons of shipping. and the new money paying for the war was chasing what was left... so prices rose. how much was money and how much was shortage... historians still argue.
P3 it hurt. in 1915, Glasgow landlords tried to push rents up by a quarter. about twenty thousand households refused to pay... and within weeks, Parliament froze rents. by 1918 the government controlled almost all food, and rationed sugar, meat and butter... so scarce food was shared, not bid up.
P4 then the war ends. businesses rush to rebuild and restock, and prices climb about fifteen percent more in 1920. then comes the cure. in April 1920 the Bank of England pushes its interest rate... the price of borrowing... up to seven percent, and holds it there for about a year.
P5 within a year, unemployment jumps from about two in a hundred to more than one in ten. how much the interest rate caused that is still argued. prices do fall... by about a fifth in two years. but the price is paid in jobs. and of half a million promised council homes, only about two hundred thousand get built.
P6 Mattei argues the interest rate wasn't just about prices. it made lenders' money worth more again... and she says putting people out of work was part of the point. fewer jobs, and workers can't ask for more. central banks see it differently: lost jobs are a cost, not the goal.
P7 in 1925 Britain went back on gold at the old pre-war rate. even today's Bank of England governor says the pound was set too high... so wages and prices had to be forced down. coal owners wanted lower pay and longer hours. in 1926 about one and a half million workers walked out. the miners held out for months... and lost.
P8 sound familiar? New Zealand, 2020. when covid hit, the Reserve Bank created about fifty-three billion dollars of new money to buy up government debt. house prices jumped about thirty percent in a year... and a Royal Commission found the pandemic response helped push them up, in part. you can't quickly make more land. by 2022, prices overall were rising seven point three percent. how much came from here, and how much from the world... nobody has worked out.
P9 then the same kind of cure. the Reserve Bank pushed its interest rate from a quarter of a percent to five and a half, in under two years. the economy shrank, twice. unemployment has climbed from three point two percent to five point six. an independent review says the support was right at first... but too strong for too long.
P10 so does new money cause inflation? it depends where and how it's spent. when it chases things that have run out, like workers in 1918, prices rise. where workers and factories sit idle, it mostly gets them working. economists who study how modern money works say the limit isn't money... it's workers, materials and machines. and today? four point one percent... two point nine without fuel.
