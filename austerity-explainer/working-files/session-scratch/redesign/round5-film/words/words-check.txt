page 3, Malina: Picture two factories. Think of each one as a whole country... all its workers, materials and machines. In the first, the machines stand still. Workers wait at the gate, hoping for a job. Call it the quiet factory. Now the government spends new money on what the factory makes. The workers go in. The machines start. More money... and more things to buy. So prices barely move.

page 4, Malina: In the second factory, every worker and every machine is already busy... working as hard as they can. No one waits at the gate. Call it the flat-out factory. The same new money is spent on what this factory makes. What happens to prices? Have a guess.

page 5, Malina: No spare workers, materials or machines... so no extra things get made. More money... and the same things to buy. So businesses can ask more... and prices go up. Most economists agree on this much. When new money is spent... and there are spare workers, materials and machines... it mostly gets more things made. When nothing is spare... it mostly pushes prices up.
