P1 So... won't creating money just cause inflation? First, the word. Inflation means prices, in general, going up over time... usually measured over a year. And in last time's story, they did go up. In Britain, everyday prices roughly doubled during the war. By 1920, they were about two and a half times what they'd been in 1914. So was it the new money? Let's go back to the shell factory.
P2 remember... a country can run out of workers, materials and machines. in the war, it did. fewer than one in a hundred people were out of work. submarines sank nearly eight million tons of shipping. and the war was paid for partly with new money and borrowing from the banks. so the new money was chasing workers, ships and food that had run out... and prices rose. how much was money and how much was shortage... historians still argue.
P3 and it hurt where people lived. in 1915, Glasgow landlords tried to push rents up by a quarter. about twenty thousand households refused to pay... and within weeks, Parliament froze rents. by 1918 the government controlled almost all food, and rationed sugar, meat and butter... so scarce food was shared out, not sold to whoever could pay most.
P4 then the war ends. businesses rush to rebuild and restock, and prices climb about fifteen percent more in 1920. then comes the cure. in April 1920 the Bank of England pushes its interest rate... the price of borrowing... up to seven percent. and holds it there for about a year. borrowing is made expensive on purpose.
P5 within a year, unemployment jumps from about two in a hundred to more than one in ten. by one estimate, everything the country makes falls by around a fifth. how much the interest rate caused that is still argued. but prices do fall... by about a fifth in two years. the price is paid in jobs. half a million council homes were promised. when the cuts came, only about two hundred thousand had been built.
P6 Mattei argues the interest rate wasn't just about prices. it made lenders' money worth more again... and she says putting people out of work was part of the point. fewer jobs, and workers can't ask for more. central banks tell it differently: higher rates cool spending, and lost jobs are a cost, not the goal. Mattei says the same pattern is back today, in Europe and America.
P7 then gold. in 1925 Britain went back on gold at the old pre-war rate. even today's Bank of England governor says the pound was set too high... so wages and prices had to be forced down to fit. coal owners wanted lower pay and longer hours. in 1926, about one and a half million workers walked out, for nine days. the miners held out for months... and lost.
P8 sound familiar? New Zealand, 2020. when covid hit, the Reserve Bank created new money and used it to buy about fifty-three billion dollars of government bonds... the IOUs a government sells when it borrows. in 2021, house prices jumped about thirty percent in a year. a Royal Commission found the pandemic response helped push them up... in part. and you can't quickly make more land.
P9 by the middle of 2022, prices overall were rising seven point three percent a year... the fastest in thirty-two years. petrol was up about a third... that came from the world. the cost of building a new house was up nearly a fifth... short supplies, labour costs, and more demand. how much came from here and how much from the world... nobody has worked out.
P10 then the same kind of cure. the Reserve Bank pushed its interest rate from a quarter of a percent up to five and a half, in under two years. the economy shrank, twice. and unemployment has climbed from three point two percent to five point six. an independent review now says the support was right at first... but too strong for too long.
P11 so does new money cause inflation? it depends where and how it's spent. in 1918 it chased workers and ships that had run out. where there are idle workers and empty factories, new spending mostly gets them working... more gets made. economists who study how modern money works say the limit isn't money. it's workers, materials and machines. and today? prices are up four point one percent... two point nine without fuel. and the Reserve Bank is raising its interest rate again.
