=== RULES 13
1 Aim the street at the story. Turn the camera so the street's long straight lines (both kerbs, the house gutters and roof edges, the pavement joints) all meet right at the edge of the main story object. In this shot that is the left edge of the notice board, beside the shut colliery gate. They must never meet on a doorway, a gap in the clouds or empty road. Where the lines meet depends only on which way the camera is turned, not on where it stands. So set it with the turn first, then move the camera or the props to set sizes and overlaps. (Frame positions here are fractions: 0 = left or top, 1 = right or bottom.)
   CHECK: Run plan3/why/tools/measure_composition.py on the test frame, or draw both kerb lines and each terrace's eaves line by hand and extend them. All crossings must fall inside one box no bigger than 0.10 of the frame width by 0.08 of its height. That box must be within 0.03 of the frame width of the board's left edge and within 0.08 of the paper. Target for this shot: 0.62 to 0.71 across, 0.42 to 0.50 down. Failure example: clip 29 frame 4, where they meet at about 0.14 across, 0.59 down, inside the colliery doorway. In Blender, each 1 degree of turn moves the meeting point about 0.027 of the frame width.
   FROM: Every line of the street leads to the spot where the shut gate meets the notice board (main reason 1).
2 Stand where a person would stand to look at the subject. Put the camera on the pavement about an arm's length in from the kerb (0.5 to 0.8 m). The eye should be 1.8 to 1.9 m above the ground the props stand on, and at least 1 m above the top of the tallest near prop, so we look into the drum, onto the seat and into the pail. Keep the camera dead level (no tilt up or down, no roll) so lamp posts, board posts, drainpipes and chimneys stay upright. Only put the camera in the middle of the road when the shot is about the road. On our street, which climbs 4 degrees, measure height from the pavement under the props, not under the camera.
   CHECK: (a) The bottom edge shows flagstones on its right part, and the near kerb crosses the bottom edge between 0.2 and 0.45 across. (b) Take the x position of the top and bottom of the lamp post, a board post and a drainpipe: none leans more than about half a degree. (c) The drum's top opening shows as an oval whose height is at least 15% of its width (the picture: about 19%), and the chair seat's top surface is visible. (d) In Blender the camera's X rotation is exactly 90 and Y is 0. Clip 29's plate camera is tilted up 8 degrees and stands in the road, which fails (b) and the 'pavement' part of (a).
   FROM: The camera was moved onto the right-hand pavement at standing eye height and held level (main reason 2).
3 Build the story props as one touching group, not as separate items on bare ground. In this shot: the drum's rim overlaps the board's lower-left corner; the chair's back sits against the board and covers the paper's lower-right corner (only the signature area, never 'PITS WILL BE CLOSED' or 'NEW TERMS'); the lantern stands on the seat; the pail stands in front of the chair's legs. The whole group fits in a box about a third of the frame wide and two-fifths of it tall.
   CHECK: Render an object index pass: a render layer in which each prop is a flat numbered colour, so its exact outline can be measured. Every story prop's outline must touch or overlap at least one other story prop, so together they form one connected cluster. The group's box is at most 0.40 of the frame width by 0.50 of its height (the picture: 0.59 to 0.97 across, 0.45 to 0.88 down). The chair covers at most the bottom 20% of the paper. Failure example: clip 29 frame 4, where the sacks, drum, coal heap and board have bare cobbles between them.
   FROM: The props near the notice touch and overlap, so they read as one group (main reason 3).
4 When two outlines meet, overlap them clearly or leave a clear gap. Never let two edges at different distances just touch. Designers call such a near-touch a 'tangent': two edges at different depths meeting exactly, which flattens the depth and makes things look stuck together.
   CHECK: From the object index pass, measure the gap or overlap for every pair of outlines. On a 1536-pixel-wide frame, an overlap must be at least 20 px and a gap at least 25 px. Flag any pair whose gap is 1 to 24 px or whose overlap is under 20 px. Known risks in this shot: the street lamp's glass against the right winding wheel (the picture: 10 to 15 px apart); the drum's rim against the board's bottom-left corner; the lantern's top ring against the board's bottom edge; the far right-hand street lamp (Blender lamp at y 39) against the board's top-left corner; the shut gate's right end against the board's left edge.
   FROM: Weak spot (edges that almost touch), plus main reason 3 (overlaps must read as deliberate).
5 Make the subject the biggest bright thing, and keep the sky dim. The notice paper must be the largest bright shape in the frame and the chair seat the next. Nothing in the top third (sky, street lamp, lit windows) may be bright over a large area. Dim the sky rather than brightening everything else. Keep the chair's brightest patch below the paper's brightest.
   CHECK: Blur the test frame slightly (radius about 1% of its width) and take the brightest 3% of pixels. At least 75% must lie inside the story group's box and at least 40% on the paper (the picture: 82% and 63%; clip 29 frame 4: 20% and 20%). On a 3 x 3 grid of the frame, each top-row cell averages no more than about 0.36 on a 0 to 1 scale (the picture: 0.33, 0.35, 0.34; clip 29: 0.43, 0.49, 0.32). The paper's average brightness is at least 1.8 times the frame's average, and the paper covers at least 2.5% of the frame. The 99th-percentile brightness inside the chair's outline is below the same value inside the paper's.
   FROM: The brightest things are all in the group and the sky is kept dim (main reason 4), plus the weak spot about the spotless white chair competing.
6 Light the notice from the people's own fire and lantern, from below. The paper should be cool at the top and warm at the bottom, with a little warm spill on the seat and on the road beside the drum. Do not light the paper from above, and do not aim a spotlight at particular words.
   CHECK: On the paper, compare the top 15% with the bottom 15%. Red minus blue (0 to 1 scale) must rise by at least 0.12, and brightness by at least 0.06 (the picture: 0.08 to 0.25, and 0.53 to 0.62; clip 29: 0.15 to 0.22 with flat brightness, which fails). measure_composition.py's 'warm light share' is 1.5 to 3% of the frame, with at least 70% of it in the group's third (the picture: 2.2%, 73% in the right third; clip 29: 0.54%). In Blender, the only warm lights near the group are inside the drum and inside the lantern, both below the paper's bottom edge.
   FROM: The warm light comes from the people's things and falls on the notice from below (supporting reason 5).
7 In the tall 9:16 frame, stack the story in two upright columns so the eye travels the full height. Here they are a 'pit' column (winding wheels at the top, gate in the middle, drum and fire at the bottom) and a 'notice' column (street lamp at the top, notice in the middle, lantern, chair and pail at the bottom). The columns stand side by side and meet where the street lines meet. The top third must hold at least one story object, not just sky.
   CHECK: List the screen centres of the named objects. Every member of a column has its centre within 0.07 of the frame width of that column's average across (the picture: pit column 0.59 to 0.60; notice column 0.79 to 0.89). The two columns are 0.15 to 0.30 of the frame width apart. Each column has a member in the top, middle and bottom thirds of the frame height.
   FROM: Two upright columns, 'pit' and 'notice', run side by side down the tall frame (supporting reason 6).
8 Sort props by meaning. The people's things (fire, chair, lantern, pail) go near the camera: big, warm, lit and touching. The work things (cart, sacks, coal tub, barrel, dustbins) go back up the street: small, in shadow, in the middle band of the frame. Nothing big and dark goes in the bottom-left, where the eye enters the frame. Story props may be bigger than life so they read on a phone. Next to a true-size drum and pail, the picture draws the chair about 1.25 to 1.4 times, the lantern about 1.5 times and the notice about 1.35 times true size. Use the same scale in every shot of this place.
   CHECK: From the object index pass: each near story prop covers at least 1.5% of the frame, except the lantern, which needs at least 0.4% and must be lit (the picture: drum 3.1%, chair 6.3%, paper 3.3%, pail 1.8%). Each work prop covers at most 2%, has an average brightness no more than 0.75 times the frame average (the picture: 0.19 to 0.23 against 0.32), and has its centre 0.45 to 0.60 down. The bottom-left quarter (left of 0.5, below 0.6 down) holds no prop larger than 1% of the frame. Clip 29 frame 4's sacks fill about 7% there, which fails.
   FROM: Props are sorted by meaning: personal ones near, big and warm, work ones far, small and dim (supporting reason 7). The scale figures come from my own measurement of the picture against our true-size props.
9 Show the missing person with one empty object made for a person: the chair. Place it directly under the notice with its back against the board, its seat turned toward the viewer and the fire, and a burning lantern on it. Keep its pale tone just under the paper's, so the eye drops from the notice to the seat and climbs back. Dirty the white paint a little so it does not look freshly painted in a sooty street.
   CHECK: The chair's centre is within 0.05 of the frame width of the paper's centre and below it (the picture: 0.835 against 0.829). The seat's top and its front edge are visible, with the front edge facing down and to the left, toward the drum. The lantern is lit and stands on the seat, not on the ground. The chair's average brightness is 0.6 to 0.95 times the paper's.
   FROM: The empty chair, back to the notice, seat to the fire, with a lit lantern on it (supporting reason 8), plus the weak spot about the white chair.
10 Keep the side away from the subject big, dim and quietly detailed: road, kerbs and house fronts with fine brick, window and cobble texture, one or two small lights (a lamp, a lit window) and a faint warm patch on the road. It must hold no object that competes. Its lines should point at the subject (rule 1). This stops the frame tipping toward the busy side, but it is only needed, not enough: clip 29 balances almost the same and still fails.
   CHECK: In measure_composition.py's visual-weight figures, the left/right split is between 40/60 and 48/52, and the centre of weight is 0.52 to 0.58 across (the picture: 43/57 and 0.55; clip 29: 46/54 and 0.53). On the quiet side, no light covers more than 0.5% of the frame or is as bright as the paper, and no prop covers more than 2%.
   FROM: The left side keeps the frame from tipping by being big, dim and textured, not by holding objects (supporting reason 9).
11 The master check: every strong signal must point at the same spot. Before any fine-tuning, list where the street lines meet, the largest bright shape, the warmest area, the biggest near props, and the story's cause and effect (the notice; the fire and the empty seat). Make them all fall in one part of the frame. If one points elsewhere (a bright sky, a big dark lump of sacks, lines meeting at a door), fix that first. It matters more than any other adjustment.
   CHECK: Mark four positions on the test frame: where the lines meet, the centre of the brightest 3%, the centre of the warm pixels, and the centre of the near story group. All four must fall inside one box at most 0.40 of the frame wide and 0.50 tall that contains the notice (the picture: all inside 0.59 to 0.97 across, 0.42 to 0.88 down). Failure example: clip 29 frame 4, where they are in three places (lines at a doorway far left, brightness in the top-middle sky, the biggest prop being the sacks at the bottom left).
   FROM: Why the picture is hard to vary: all the strong signals agree on one corner, and the 'balance' is that agreement, not an even left-right weight.
12 Move the camera without turning it. Slide it in a straight line toward the subject at a constant height, with no pan, tilt or roll during the move, easing in and out, over about a metre, and end on the composed frame rather than past it. Because the meeting point of the street lines depends only on the camera's turn, a move with no turn keeps the lines pointing at the subject in every frame. A turn during the move slides the meeting point across the frame. That is what made clip 29's move feel odd: between 1.5 s and 4 s its meeting point slides from about 0.28 to 0.14 across, toward a doorway at the edge, while the winding gear slides out of frame.
   CHECK: Measure where the lines meet on the first, middle and last frames: it moves no more than 0.02 of the frame width. Run the checks for rules 3, 4, 5, 8 and 13 on every 10th frame: the group stays connected, no new near-touches appear, the paper stays the largest bright shape, and no story prop crosses 0.95 across or the bottom edge. In Blender the camera's rotation has no keyframes, only its location does, and its Z value is the same at both ends.
   FROM: Weak spot (the arrangement works only from this spot), main reason 1 (lines must point at the subject), and the comparison with clip 29's move.
13 Leave room for phone buttons and titles. Keep the parts that must be read (the notice text, the lantern) left of 0.90 across. Only non-essential edges (chair back, pail) may reach 0.95. TikTok, Reels and Shorts put their buttons down the right edge from a little above the middle to near the bottom. Put titles in the dim sky at the top left, away from the street lamp, which is the brightest point in the top half.
   CHECK: The paper's right edge is at or below 0.90 across (the picture: 0.91), and the chair and pail are at or below 0.95 (the picture: 0.96 to 0.97, which fails). A title box in the top-left contains no pixel brighter than about 0.5 and stays at least 0.05 of the frame width clear of the street lamp's glow.
   FROM: Weak spots: the right edge is tight, and the street lamp may catch the first glance.

=== CAMERA
A slow, straight push toward the notice with no turning. Move the camera 1.2 m over about 6 seconds, easing in and out, from (3.18, 15.40, 3.52) to the composed position (3.25, 16.60, 3.52), along the line that points at the paper's centre. Keep the height and the rotation (90, 0, 5.9 degrees) fixed, and keyframe the location only. Because the camera never turns, the street lines meet at the same spot (0.66 across, 0.44 down) in every frame. The group is 4.4 to 5.6 m away and only about 1.2 m deep, so the overlaps barely change: the drum's rim stays over the board's corner, the chair back stays on the paper's corner and the lantern stays against the board. The notice grows from about 2.0% to 2.9% of the frame and stays the brightest shape, and all props stay between 0.49 and 0.93 across. Stop exactly on the composed frame, because pushing 0.5 m further crowds the pail and chair to about 0.95 across and the bottom edge. Then cut to the notice close-up. In the first second, watch the bottom-right corner for the door step of right-row cottage 05.
