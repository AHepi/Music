In order: (1) bridge: greyer base, more moss and mould on plank ends and shaded sides, varied knots per plank; (2) pond: bigger brighter pads, clearer shallow edge, larger ripple rings, bigger lily flowers; (3) tame the stickers: lantern cap moss smaller and darker, rock line colours at a third; (4) maple: lift the upper pad colour, open the branch gaps; (5) moss cushions: vary size and cut count; (6) pine fringe shorter; (7) make pegs, drip stains, flowers and leaves 30 to 50 percent bigger. Run the four stages again, check ownership as before, redraw hero and veranda at 2400x1600, and re-measure squint and the 12 of 16 reading test. No new paid image calls are needed. Do not push anything toward realism: keep the value range and detail energy where they are now.
-- overall
The garden is clearly better and the art style is kept: the light, palette, silhouettes and value range are the same as before, and the new details are drawn chunky and clean (carved plank grain, square posts, tile-end discs, moss caps, clean cracks, low-poly iris), not photographic. The bridge now has a logic that makes sense, the deck is a big step up, the roof ends and lantern cracks read, and the worn path and seed-head tufts show. What is not there yet: the pond still looks like before at normal size, the planks are too clean and pale with almost no moss, the same knot is stamped on most boards, the moss cushions repeat like bean bags, the maple became darker and heavier, and several small features (pegs, drip stains, flowers) are invisible at 1200 px, so a fresh reviewer would likely name 7 to 11 of the 16 features, not 12. The builders' numbers held up where I re-checked them (value range, squint miss, detail energy, budget, ledger); the one claim that did not hold is the 9.8 percent moss and mould on the planks. I recommend one cheap fix round before showing it as finished. I tested only by looking at the finals and close-ups and by recomputing picture numbers; I did not re-run the structure, walkability, ownership or normal-map direction tests, and I was not blind to the feature list.
