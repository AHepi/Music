## Things I could not verify (kept out of the film)

- A wartime British unemployment figure (often quoted as under 1% in 1916-18). The 1914-1918 Online
  encyclopedia blocked automated reading. Not used.
- The exact Brussels resolution wording about banks being "freed from political pressure". The official
  proceedings could not be opened. The film paraphrases The Conversation's summary instead.
- Whether "work more, consume less" was a literal slogan of the period, and in which language. Used
  only as a summary of the message.

## Traps

- The author's name is Clara E. Mattei. "Clara Matteo" is wrong.
- Do not call the film's money view "MMT" anywhere in the film (the user's instruction).
- Do not say Britain "left the gold standard" in 1914 as a legal fact: convertibility was never legally
  suspended. Say paper notes replaced gold coins.
- Do not say troops fired on the Glasgow crowd. Police and crowd clashed; troops and tanks came after.
- The Sankey Commission's majority favoured nationalisation; it was not unanimous.
- "About half a million" occupied Italian factories combines 400,000 metalworkers and 100,000 others.
- Mattei's claims about purpose (power, class) are her interpretation. Attribute them to her.
