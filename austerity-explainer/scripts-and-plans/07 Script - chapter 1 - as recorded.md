## Page 1 - The speech (starts at 0:01)

You've heard the speech. Times are tough. Tighten your belts. Cut spending, push up interest rates,
hold wages down. Sounds like common sense... But austerity has a birthday. Economist Clara E. Mattei
dug through the archives and found it, right after the First World War. And she argues it was never
really about budgets. It was about stopping something. [C1-01, C1-02]

**On the page:** a clipping headed BUDGET SPEECH with the quote typed as she says it; three slips:
CUT SPENDING / PUSH UP INTEREST RATES / HOLD WAGES DOWN. Red pen: COMMON SENSE? The cut-out word
AUSTERITY, and a red-pen birthday candle on it. A typed name card: ECONOMIST / CLARA E. MATTEI /
THE CAPITAL ORDER, 2022. A red stamp: BORN AFTER 1918. On "budgets", the red pen strikes out BUDGET.
Last: STOP WHAT?

## Page 2 - The war (0:25)

Picture Britain in 1915. The war is burning through shells faster than factories can make them. And
the government doesn't run out of money. It runs out of shells. So it takes charge: what factories
make, what things cost, what workers are paid. Italy does the same. And people notice. If the economy
can be organised for war... why not for what people need, instead of just for profit?
[C1-03, C1-04, C1-07, C1-29]

**On the page:** BRITAIN 1915. An engraving of five artillery shells, cut one shell at a time. A note:
SHORT OF: MONEY (struck out) / SHELLS (circled). A card: SO IT TAKES CHARGE OF: WHAT FACTORIES MAKE /
WHAT THINGS COST / WHAT WORKERS ARE PAID, each ticked. A slip: ITALY DOES THE SAME. A card: ORGANISED
FOR WAR... / WHY NOT FOR WHAT PEOPLE NEED, / NOT JUST FOR PROFIT?

## Page 3 - Britain after the war (0:51)

When the war ends, workers don't want to go back. In Britain, union membership doubles, to eight
million. In Glasgow, sixty thousand people pack a square, demanding a shorter working week. The
government sends in troops. And tanks. Coal miners demand public ownership of the mines. A royal
commission backs them. The government ignores it. [C1-08, C1-10, C1-11]

**On the page:** a bar chart drawn to scale, union members in millions: 1914 4.1, 1918 6.5, 1920 8.3,
with DOUBLED in red pen. A card: GLASGOW / 31 JANUARY 1919 / 60,000 PEOPLE / FOR A SHORTER WORKING
WEEK, a red stamp TROOPS SENT IN and red pen AND TANKS. A card: COAL MINERS WANT / PUBLIC OWNERSHIP OF
THE MINES / ROYAL COMMISSION AGREES (ticked) / GOVERNMENT:, and a red stamp IGNORED.

## Page 4 - Italy (1:14)

In Italy, it goes further. In 1919, more than a million workers go on strike. In 1920, half a million
take over their factories. [C1-13]

**On the page:** the cut-out word ITALY. An engraving of a factory with a saw-tooth roof and a
chimney. A card: 1919: OVER 1,000,000 ON STRIKE (the number circled) / 1920: ABOUT 500,000 TAKE OVER
THEIR FACTORIES. As she says "take over", the red pen raises a red flag on the factory roof.

## Page 5 - The capital order (1:25)

Now look at it from the owners' side. This isn't just about pay. It threatens what Mattei calls the
capital order: a few people own the land, the factories and the mines... and everyone else must work
for a wage to survive. If workers can run things themselves, that order could fall. [C1-15]

**On the page:** the test picture you saw: the engraved temple, THE CAPITAL ORDER in cut-out letters,
MATTEI'S TERM, the two cards (A FEW OWN THE LAND, FACTORIES AND MINES / EVERYONE ELSE WORKS FOR A WAGE
TO SURVIVE) with red arrows to the two columns, a red crack, COULD FALL?

## Page 6 - The experts (1:43)

So the experts get together. Brussels, 1920: thirty-nine countries send delegates, mostly bankers and
treasury officials. Guess who isn't in the room? Not one trade unionist. Genoa, 1922: same advice. And
it's simple. Balance the budget. Cut public spending. Stop creating new money. Hand interest rates to
central banks, out of reach of voters. And get back on gold. [C1-16, C1-17, C1-18, C1-19]

**On the page:** the cut-out words THE EXPERTS. A card: BRUSSELS, 1920 / 39 COUNTRIES / MOSTLY BANKERS
AND TREASURY OFFICIALS. Red pen: TRADE UNIONISTS: 0. A card: GENOA, 1922: SAME ADVICE, then the advice
typed and ticked line by line, with OUT OF REACH OF VOTERS underlined.

## Page 7 - Three parts (2:10)

Mattei says the plan has three parts. Spending: cut public services, and tax everyday purchases
instead of wealth. Money: make borrowing expensive, so businesses slow down and jobs disappear. Work:
cut wages, sell off what the public owns, weaken the unions. [C1-21]

**On the page:** MATTEI SAYS, the cut-out words THREE PARTS, and three luggage tags: SPENDING (CUT
PUBLIC SERVICES / TAX PURCHASES, NOT WEALTH), MONEY (MAKE BORROWING EXPENSIVE / BUSINESSES SLOW, JOBS
DISAPPEAR), WORK (CUT WAGES / SELL OFF WHAT THE PUBLIC OWNS / WEAKEN THE UNIONS).

## Page 8 - The official story (2:30)

The official story sounds reasonable. Prices are rising, war debts are huge, and a government, like a
household, has to balance its books. Right? But economists who study how modern money works say a
country that issues its own money isn't a household. It can't run out of its own money. It can run out
of workers, materials and machines, as the war just showed. And gold? Gold was a limit they chose.
Going back to it meant forcing wages and prices down. [C1-27, C1-28, C1-26, C1-24, C1-25]

**On the page:** THE OFFICIAL STORY: PRICES ARE RISING / WAR DEBTS ARE HUGE / LIKE A HOUSEHOLD, /
BALANCE THE BOOKS, and red pen RIGHT? A card: ECONOMISTS WHO STUDY MODERN MONEY: A COUNTRY THAT ISSUES
ITS OWN MONEY: CAN'T RUN OUT OF MONEY / CAN RUN OUT OF WORKERS, MATERIALS AND MACHINES. On "isn't a
household", the red pen strikes out LIKE A HOUSEHOLD. An engraving of three gold bars; the red pen
writes A LIMIT THEY CHOSE and draws a padlock on the gold; then WAGES and PRICES with arrows down.

## Page 9 - Why (3:00)

So why do it? Ask Mattei, and her answer is blunt: power. Take away the alternatives, like public
housing and help for the unemployed, and people must take whatever job is going, at whatever wage. The
message to workers: work more, consume less. [C1-22, C1-12, C1-21, C1-23]

**On the page:** SO WHY DO IT? / MATTEI'S ANSWER: and the cut-out word POWER. Two cards, PUBLIC HOUSING
and HELP FOR THE UNEMPLOYED, each crossed out in red as she names it. A brown pay envelope: ANY JOB /
ANY WAGE. THE MESSAGE TO WORKERS: and the cut-out words WORK MORE, CONSUME LESS.

## Page 10 - Sound familiar? (3:19)

That's the argument Mattei makes. Austerity was never really about the books. It was about power. Sound
familiar? But wait. If a country can't run out of its own money... won't creating it just cause
inflation? That's next. [C1-22]

**On the page:** MATTEI'S ARGUMENT: NOT ABOUT THE BOOKS. ABOUT POWER. Red pen: SOUND FAMILIAR? A card:
BUT WAIT. IF A COUNTRY CAN'T RUN OUT OF ITS OWN MONEY... WON'T CREATING IT JUST CAUSE, then the cut-out
word INFLATION? A slip: NEXT: WHAT REALLY CAUSES INFLATION? A red stamp: CHAPTER 1, WHERE IT CAME FROM.

## A quick listener test (from your toolkit)

After a first watch, ask someone (or yourself):

1. In one sentence, why does Mattei say austerity was invented?

## Traps

- "Mattei says", "Mattei calls", "her answer", "the argument Mattei makes" mark her interpretation.
  The words on the pages do the same (MATTEI'S TERM, MATTEI SAYS, MATTEI'S ANSWER, MATTEI'S ARGUMENT).
- "Not one trade unionist" is about Brussels only; Genoa was a meeting of heads of government.
- The chart is drawn to scale (4.1, 6.5 and 8.3 million). If a figure changes, the bar must change too.
- The words on the pages are shortened from what Malina says in a few places (TAX PURCHASES, NOT
  WEALTH; ANY JOB, ANY WAGE). They must never say more than she does or than the research supports.
- The times above come from the recordings. Re-recording any page changes every time after it.
