# 03 Script - chapter 1 - where austerity came from

**What this is:** the narration for the test chapter, page by page, with what gets drawn while
each part is spoken. About 450 words, so about 3 minutes 20 seconds at the sample voices' pace.
The film never says "MMT". Mattei's own interpretations are said as hers.

Source for the story: Clara E. Mattei, *The Capital Order* (2022). Every fact is checked in
"02 Research - chapter 1 - where austerity came from.md"; the codes in brackets (C1-10 and so on)
point to the entry that backs it.

---

## Page 1 - Title

**Narrator:** Austerity. Cut public spending. Make borrowing dear. Hold wages down. It's sold as
common sense. But the economist Clara E. Mattei traced modern austerity back to a particular
moment: the years right after the First World War. [C1-01, C1-02]

**On the page:** the word AUSTERITY pasted up in cut-out letters, one at a time. Three typed slips
arrive with the three phrases. A red-pen note: "SOLD AS COMMON SENSE". A typed card: "CLARA E.
MATTEI - THE CAPITAL ORDER - 2022".

## Page 2 - The war economy

**Narrator:** During the war, the state took charge of the economy, in Britain and in Italy. It
told factories what to make, and set prices and wages. In 1915, Britain didn't run out of money.
It ran out of shells, so it organised factories to make more. People saw that production could
serve need, not just profit. [C1-03, C1-04, C1-07, C1-29]

**On the page:** an engraving of artillery shells on a torn card, "1915". A typed list ticks off
"WHAT FACTORIES MADE / PRICES / WAGES". Red pen: "NOT SHORT OF MONEY. SHORT OF SHELLS."

## Page 3 - Workers want more (Britain)

**Narrator:** When the war ended, working people wanted that power to stay. In Britain, union
membership doubled, to over eight million. In Glasgow, sixty thousand people rallied for a shorter
working week, and the government sent in troops and tanks. Miners asked for the mines to be
publicly owned. A royal commission backed them. The government ignored it. [C1-08, C1-10, C1-11]

**On the page:** a bar chart drawn to scale, union members in millions: 1914 4.1, 1918 6.5,
1920 8.3; red pen "DOUBLED". Typed index cards: "GLASGOW - 31 JAN 1919 - 60,000 RALLY - TROOPS
AND TANKS SENT IN" and "COAL - 1919 - ROYAL COMMISSION: PUBLIC OWNERSHIP - GOVERNMENT: IGNORED".

## Page 4 - Italy

**Narrator:** In Italy, over a million workers went on strike in 1919. In 1920, about half a
million occupied their factories. [C1-13]

**On the page:** an engraved factory. Typed: "ITALY - 1919: OVER 1,000,000 ON STRIKE" and
"1920: ABOUT 500,000 OCCUPY THEIR FACTORIES", the number circled in red.

## Page 5 - The capital order

**Narrator:** To owners, and the economists who advised them, this threatened what Mattei calls
the capital order: a few own the factories, the mines and the land, and everyone else must work
for a wage to live. [C1-15]

**On the page:** an engraved temple front. On its roof: THE CAPITAL ORDER. One column: "A FEW OWN
THE FACTORIES, MINES AND LAND". The other: "EVERYONE ELSE WORKS FOR A WAGE". Red pen: "MATTEI'S
TERM". (This is the test picture.)

## Page 6 - The experts meet

**Narrator:** So the experts met. In Brussels in 1920, delegates from thirty-nine countries,
mostly bankers and treasury officials, agreed on what governments should do. No trade unionists
were there. Genoa, in 1922, repeated the advice. Balance the budget. Cut public spending. Stop
creating new money. Let central banks set interest rates, away from elected politicians. And go
back to gold. [C1-16, C1-17, C1-18, C1-19]

**On the page:** typed notes "BRUSSELS 1920 - 39 COUNTRIES - BANKERS AND TREASURY OFFICIALS -
TRADE UNIONISTS: NONE", then "GENOA 1922". A numbered list typed line by line as each piece of
advice is spoken, each ticked in red.

## Page 7 - Three kinds

**Narrator:** Mattei sorts austerity into three kinds that work together. Spending: cut public
services, and tax everyday purchases more than wealth. Money: make borrowing dear, so businesses
slow and jobs disappear. Work: cut wages, sell off public enterprises, weaken unions. [C1-21]

**On the page:** three tags hung from a twine line: SPENDING, MONEY, WORK, each filling with two
typed lines as it is spoken.

## Page 8 - The official reason, and the money view

**Narrator:** The official reasons sounded sensible: prices were rising, war debts were huge, and
a government, like a household, should balance its books. But economists who study how modern
money works point out that a country which issues its own money is not a household. It can't run
out of its own money. What it can run out of is workers, materials and machines. Gold was a limit
they chose, and going back to it meant forcing wages and prices down. [C1-27, C1-28, C1-26,
C1-24, C1-25]

**On the page:** two cards. "A HOUSEHOLD: CAN RUN OUT OF MONEY." / "A COUNTRY THAT ISSUES ITS OWN
MONEY: CAN'T. IT CAN RUN OUT OF WORKERS, MATERIALS, MACHINES." Then an engraved gold bar, a red-pen
padlock drawn on it: "A LIMIT THEY CHOSE".

## Page 9 - Why

**Narrator:** So why choose austerity? Mattei's answer: to restore owners' power over workers. Take
away the alternatives, like public housing and support for the unemployed, and people must accept
whatever wage is offered. The message to workers: work more, consume less. [C1-22, C1-12, C1-21,
C1-23]

**On the page:** a brown wage envelope pinned to the page. Small cards "PUBLIC HOUSING" and
"SUPPORT FOR THE UNEMPLOYED" are crossed out in red. Cut-out letters paste up WORK MORE, CONSUME
LESS.

## Page 10 - Close

**Narrator:** In Mattei's telling, austerity was never mainly about money. It was about power.
Next: where it was first tried, and what it did to people. [C1-22]

**On the page:** typed "NOT ABOUT MONEY. ABOUT POWER." A rubber stamp: CHAPTER 1. Typed: "NEXT:
WHERE IT WAS TRIED".

## Traps

- Say "Mattei calls", "Mattei's answer", "in Mattei's telling" where the claim is her
  interpretation. Do not turn these into plain statements of fact.
- Genoa was a meeting of heads of government; only Brussels was "mostly bankers and treasury
  officials". The script keeps them apart.
- "Mattei" should sound like "mah-TAY". Listen for it in the voice samples.
- Pages are timed to the recorded narration, not to these word counts.
