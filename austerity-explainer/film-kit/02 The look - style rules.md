# The look: style rules

**What this is:** what the paper pop-up look is, in the user's own words and as rules, so a new
series looks like the austerity chapter. The user approved this look after seeing a test page
("This is great!") and then the whole chapter ("This is fantastic.").

## The user's words (quoted exactly)

When asking for the look, the user showed four pictures from the "Briefed History" channel (painted,
semi-realistic stills in teal and amber) and wrote:

> I meant a style more like this. With still images with graphics like:
> - Flip into frame like a pop up book.
> - Transitions like flipping the page of a book.
> - speaking like a person is moving a fold of paper up and down in a flip book.
> - Occasional graphics like figures, objects and texts crashing onto the page to dramatize a point.
> - Just as an example: there could be civilians happy and cheering, then a tank crashes into frame, symbolising power and oppression.
> - How words appear depend on context. They should be minimal. They aren't dialogue or subtitles. They function more like hooks to emphasize the main points.
> - Words generally only appear to aid memory. They should pop into frame, and be unstylized.
> You may need to do some research on this particular style and best practices. It's important that they images are not animated like a cartoon. They should be animated like pieces of paper or cardboard being moved around on a surface without a hand. No hands!!!!

## The rules that make it read as paper

| Rule | How the kit does it |
|---|---|
| Still painted pictures, never cartoon movement | Paintings are never redrawn or bent; they only fold, slide, drop, hop, rock and turn as stiff paper. |
| No hands, ever | Nothing is pushed by a hand; pieces move on their own hinges and paths. |
| Pieces flip up like a pop-up book | `popUp`: a piece hinged on its bottom edge folds up from lying flat with a quick settle. |
| Pages turn like a book | The last sheet of a page peels from its bottom-right corner over a rolling curl, showing the plain paper back, 0.3 s after the page's last word. Pieces fold down flat first, front ones first. |
| A speaking figure moves a fold of paper | A jaw cut out of the face slides down and up with the narrator's loudness, showing a dark mouth behind (no lip drawing). It looks like a cut-out puppet. |
| Things crash in to make a point | `dropIn` with a camera shake and a burst of dust; a door slams up; a word crashes down. Use it sparingly, for the points that matter. A crash can be a symbol (the tank stands for power) but the narration must not claim more than the research says. |
| Show the cut | Every piece keeps a narrow off-white paper margin with smooth scissor-cut edges. |
| One lamp, soft shadows | One lamp from the upper left; every piece stands a little in front of its sheet and casts a soft shadow on it. |
| Layers like a cardboard stage | Pieces stand at different distances: back crowd, soldiers, tank, front crowd. |
| A living camera, never a busy one | A slow push in on each sheet, a slight drift, easing back on page turns, a short shake on impacts. |
| Words: few, plain, hooks for memory | Plain heavy type (Inter, weight 800), off-white, no outline or effect; each word is a paper piece that pops up and folds away. Never dialogue, never subtitles. Usually short forms of what is said: 8 MILLION, 60,000, IGNORED, NOT A HOUSEHOLD, INFLATION?. Words stand close to their sheet (0.55) so their shadow doesn't read as a second word. |
| Printed things are objects, not hooks | A card, sign, poster or label printed on an object (BACK TO GOLD, SOLD, TAX, a book's title) belongs to the object and is drawn in dark ink on card. |
| Change shown by swapping cut-outs | A pay packet "shrinks" by being swapped for a smaller one; a price tag "grows" by being swapped for bigger ones. Nothing stretches. |

## The paintings

- Painterly 2D illustration in the look of a modern animated history documentary: semi-realistic with
  slightly stylised, expressive proportions, visible brush strokes, rich detail, cinematic lighting.
- Restrained palette: deep teal-blue shadows, cold blue-grey midtones, warm amber lamplight, small
  touches of dull brick red. Tense, uneasy mood.
- Backgrounds are tall (9:16), seen at standing eye level, with the lower quarter empty so pieces can
  stand in front. Figures and objects are painted alone on plain grey and cut out.
- No writing, numbers, logos, emblems or symbols in any painting; words are added by the kit.
- The exact style words are in `04 Painting prompts.md`.

## Things the austerity series decided (keep unless the user says otherwise)

- No drawn portraits of real people. Real people appear as their name or their work (the author
  appeared only as her name printed on a plain book). Invented figures stand for groups.
- Every painting is checked for wrong-era details, symbols that could be misread (a hooked-cross-like
  shape, a real movement's flag), and flags before it goes in.
- Tall phone screen, 1080 x 1920, 30 frames a second. Narration by the user's ElevenLabs voice Malina
  over a quiet soundtrack; captions as a separate file, not burned into the picture.
- An author's interpretations are said as theirs ("Mattei argues"), not as plain fact.

## Traps

- Moving a painting in a way paper cannot move (stretching, bending a figure's arm) breaks the look.
- Too many crashes stop meaning anything; save them for the strongest points.
- A word that floats far from its sheet casts a shadow that reads as a ghost word.
- Words that repeat the narration in full become subtitles; keep them to a few words.
