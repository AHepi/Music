# 11 Research - paper pop-up style

**What this is:** what makes paper cut-out and pop-up animation look real, gathered after you asked
for still painted pictures moved like pieces of paper or card on a surface, with no hands (log 11),
and how the test of page 3 puts each point into practice.

## What makes it read as paper

| Point | Where it comes from | How the test does it |
|---|---|---|
| Show that the pieces are cut out; don't hide the edges | Paper-cut style guides call this "embracing the seam" ([atlabs: paper cutout animation](https://www.atlabs.ai/styles/paper-cutout-animation)) | Every figure and object keeps a narrow off-white paper margin, with smooth scissor-like edges |
| Soft shadows under every piece | Same source: soft shadows "make the pieces look pinned" to a board | One lamp, upper left; each piece stands a little in front of its page and casts a soft shadow on it |
| Movement in flat layers, like a cardboard stage | Same source; cut-out animation is flat pieces moved frame by frame ([Wikipedia: Cutout animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutout_animation)) | Pieces stand in layers at different distances from the page: soldiers, back crowd, tank, front crowd |
| Pop-ups fold up from a hinge at their bottom edge | Pop-up book animations put each piece's pivot on its bottom edge and rotate it upright; a wrong pivot makes it swing from the wrong place ([ITBusiness: pop-up book in After Effects](https://itbusiness.ca/?p=12137)) | Each piece hinges on its bottom edge and folds up from flat with a small, quick settle |
| A little camera movement, a shake for impact | Same source: a slow camera move and a small shake hide seams and add life | Slow drift in; a short shake when the tank lands and when the report is slapped down |
| Mouths as a separate jointed piece | Cut-out figures are jointed with pins so a part moves on a fixed line; Terry Gilliam's Monty Python cut-outs used top light and moving jaws ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutout_animation); Gilliam's 1974 BBC tutorial, via [Make](https://makezine.com/article/craft/how-to_cut-out_animation_with/)) | The miner's jaw is cut out of his face and slides down and up on Malina's syllables, showing a dark mouth behind |
| No hands | Your instruction | Nothing is ever pushed by a hand: pieces move on their own hinges and paths |

## How a page turns

A book page turns from its corner: the corner lifts toward you, rolls over, shows its plain back,
and uncovers the next page. The test does this by rolling the page over a moving cylinder of paper
from its bottom-right corner toward the top left; the rolled part throws a shadow on the page
underneath. Before the turn, the pieces fold down flat one after another, as pop-ups do when a book
closes.

## Words

Your rules: few words, never dialogue or subtitles, only to help memory; they pop into frame and are
plain. The test uses a plain heavy typeface (Inter), off-white, with no outline or effect. Each word
is a paper piece like the others: it folds up from flat and casts a small shadow.

## What could not be checked

- Gilliam's own tutorial is a video; its contents could not be read, only the pages about it.
- No source gives numbers for timing; the settle of a pop-up (about a quarter of a second) and the
  size of shadows were judged by eye on test frames.

## Traps

- A flat sheet facing the lamp casts no shadow unless it is set to cast from both of its faces (see
  Lessons 20). Check for shadows with the lamp alone before judging a scene.
- Painted figures from different requests drift in style (the tank came out more like a 3D render
  than the crowds). Grade them toward the backgrounds, and ask for the same painting style each time.
- Video models and image models add details nobody asked for: check every picture closely for
  symbols, flags and wrong-era machines before it goes in.
