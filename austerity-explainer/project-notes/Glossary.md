# Glossary

What this file is: the terms from the project's authority source, with plain meanings, plus where
to find that source.

## Authority source

- **Clara E. Mattei, *The Capital Order: How Economists Invented Austerity and Paved the Way to
  Fascism*** (University of Chicago Press, 2022). Not held in this project; this session works from
  the author's interviews, her 2018 journal article and reviews (listed in the research files).
  It stays frozen: nothing in the project rewrites or reinterprets it beyond what the research files
  record.

## Terms

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Austerity | Government policies that cut public spending, make borrowing dearer and hold down wages, presented as tightening the belt. |
| The capital order | Mattei's name for a society where a few own the farms, factories and mines, and everyone else must work for a wage to live. |
| Fiscal austerity | The spending kind: cut public services and shift taxes onto everyday purchases rather than wealth. |
| Monetary austerity | The money kind: high interest rates and falling prices, which slow businesses and cost jobs. |
| Industrial austerity | The work kind: wage cuts, selling public enterprises, weaker unions and labour protections. |
| Technocracy | Rule by experts, who present their choices as neutral science rather than politics. |
| War collectivism | Reviewers' name for wartime state control of the economy (production, prices, wages). |
| Biennio Rosso | "The two red years" in Italy, 1919-1920: mass strikes and factory occupations. |
| Gold standard | A system where a country's money is tied to a fixed amount of gold. |
| Brussels and Genoa conferences | International meetings of financial experts in 1920 and 1922 that set out austerity's rules. |
| Treasury view | The British Treasury's 1920s belief that public spending only crowds out private spending. |

## Traps

- "War collectivism" is a reviewer's label; do not put it in Mattei's mouth as a quote.
- Do not define "the capital order" as only "capitalism": Mattei's term stresses the need to work
  for wages.
