# Make a painted place

What this is: five things and a loop. Read `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/look/The look.md` (one page) before building; everything else is in the tools' own help (`--help`).

Every command below is written for the `blender` program. Where only the `bpy` Python module is installed (no `blender` command), run the same script with Python and drop the `--`: `python3 SCRIPT ARGS` instead of `blender -b --python SCRIPT -- ARGS`.

## The loop

1. **Build the shapes, your way.** Plain boxes and cylinders are enough; the dressing and the light do the rest. Put the role in each object's name (`left terrace 03 wall`, `window lit`, `door wood`, `kerb stone`, `lamp post iron`, `lamp glass`, `puddle water`, `roof slate`, `chimney smoke`; the full list is `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/materials/Roles.md`). Lay the place along Y, ground at Z = 0, in metres. Put the camera where a standing person would stand (eye 1.65 m, 32 mm, tall frame) looking down the place's long line so the lines meet in the upper middle; one thing of interest centred, something detailed along the bottom edge. Save it: `PLACE.blend`.
2. **Dress it:** `blender -b --python ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/materials/apply_by_role.py -- --scene PLACE.blend --save-as DRESSED.blend --report dressing.json`. One call does all of this from your shapes and names, without being asked:
   - gives every object a proven material by its role;
   - lays real setts on a flat road and worn flags on a flat pavement;
   - builds every flat stone wall up into courses of chipped ashlar, stopping at your windows and doors so they sit in deep reveals, and gives each window jambs, a lintel, a sill and a sash, each wall bands, a cornice and corner stones; roof slabs get slate course lines;
   - weathers timber with photographed surfaces (free Poly Haven scans in `materials/textures/`): big timber becomes weathered grey boards, each board its own patch and shade, with rain streaks and one faded paint per building peeling off; a deck or floor laid as one flat slab becomes weathered planks; trunks get wet bark (ringed for palms); a plain timber wall is laid as overlapping boards with casings, corner boards and nails that bleed rust; doors, shutters and frames keep the street's dark peeling paint. Sand is a photographed damp sand, and the street's stone (walls, dressings, flags, rocks) gets the grain of a real sandstone over its own colours;
   - lays the details where the place's own shape says they belong: puddles placed where a lamp or lit window will mirror in them, water in the gutters, damp at every wall's foot, soot and run-off stains, rust under iron and rusted pipes, weeds and moss at wall feet, along kerbs and in the joints between setts, the place's rubbish along gutters, wall feet and doorsteps (coal, cinders, straw and paper in a street; shells, coral and seaweed on a shore), whitened doorsteps;
   - looks at the place through the camera and dresses whatever it sees standing on the ground, whatever its shape: damp, rot and mould up the foot of timber, paint flakes and splinters in front of it, weeds, moss and leaves at the feet of posts, rocks and steps, sand drifted against anything standing in sand, and what trees drop (dead fronds and coconuts under palms, leaves under other trees);
   - gives water its edges wherever it meets ground: by the sea a dark glossy wet band, foam lace and a wandering swash line, broken surf further out, a wrack line of seaweed, sticks, husk fibre, shells and a few of today's scraps, and bleached driftwood; by a pond or river a wet mossy bank and leaves against it and floating near it; rocks, posts and hulls in either wet to the high-water line.
   Read the report: every object should have a role, and the right one (`by_role`); fix names for the ones in `unassigned` or wrongly placed and run it again on `PLACE.blend` (running it again is safe: a role the dressing worked out is worked out again from the new name). Dials for the details (0 none, 1 the street, 2 twice): `--wet` for puddles, gutter water, the wet band by water, foam and swash, and things wet to the tide line; `--grime` for damp, soot, run-off stains, rust and rot up timber; `--growth` for weeds and moss, mould on timber and moss along banks; `--litter` for leaves, rubbish, flakes, sand drifts, the wrack line, driftwood and what trees drop. `--today` adds today's scraps (plastic, rope ends, a sandal) to a tide line when the request is set now. `--no-setts`, `--no-facade`, `--no-boards`, `--no-details`, `--no-scans` switch a part off. Nothing grows or gathers indoors: a room with a ceiling stays clean at its feet.
3. **Light it and draw it small:** `blender -b --python ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/stage/render_small.py -- --scene DRESSED.blend --out round-1.png [--rain]`. About two minutes at 540 x 960 on a plain computer; seconds on a rented card. The look stage finds every glowing window and lamp and puts a real light at it, adds the sky rig and the haze, sets the colour and the finish. It writes `round-1 - dials.json` beside the picture.
4. **Measure, then judge blind.** `python3 ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/look/measure.py round-1.png` says whether the picture sits inside the look's bands and names the dial if not. Then `python3 ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/look/judge.py round-1.png --out judge-1 --request "the person's request, word for word"` and start the `blind-judge` agent with only this sentence: "Judge the packet at judge-1." The judge gives two verdicts: does it have the look, and does it show what the person asked for. Never give the judge your report, your scene or your reasoning. Save its answer as `judge-1/verdict.md`.
5. **One fix pass.** REQUEST fixes (the wrong place, period, climate, building or use; something asked for that is missing) change the build script first: a picture with the look but not the place has failed. DETAIL and SURFACE fixes ("too clean", "no puddles", "more weeds", "the timber looks new") change step 2's `--grime`, `--wet`, `--growth`, `--litter` or a role word. DIAL fixes change a number in step 3's command (`--exposure`, `--window-light`, `--lamp-light`, `--fire-light`, `--sky-light`, `--amber-reflection`, `--haze`, `--painterly`, `--no-haze`, `--person-camera`; the coal mine with few windows wanted window light 2.0, a narrow lane with ninety glowing panes wanted 0.3, lamp light 0.6, sky light 1.6 and no off-frame amber light); SHAPE fixes change the build script. Make every fix in one sweep, then draw `round-2.png` once. If either verdict (look or request) is "no", judge round 2 the same way; if both are "nearly" or "yes", stop here: a third pass is where the last plugin's costs and procedure grew.
6. **Report** in plain words: the verdict, what you fixed, the measures of the last picture, the time each picture took, and what you did not do. Attach the pictures.

The full-size picture (1080 x 1920, 64 samples) is drawn once, at the end, with the same dials: `--width 1080 --height 1920 --samples 64`. Where lamps, windows or fires are lit, the picture is Cycles (the look stage sets it); on a rented card if one is set up, otherwise here.

## Worked example

`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/examples/lane-blockout/` builds a grey-box lane of 674 named boxes and cylinders; the dressing gave all 673 meshes a material from their names and laid 12,279 setts on the flat road; the look stage lit it; its `What is here.md` shows the rounds and the measures. `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/examples/coal-mine/` shows the look stage dropped onto a place built by someone else with its own materials: as it was, the defaults, the one fix pass (exposure 1.0, window light 2.0).

## What stays out

- No gate stops a picture. A picture that fails the measures is still drawn and still judged; the judge's words are the test.
- Do not paint surfaces in code, model trees from blobs, or stamp a painted hill band behind the place: a plain world and the haze do the distance. If the place needs a far hill, build its shape and give it the `earth` role.
- Do not model ashlar, sills, sashes, weatherboards, puddles, weeds, moss, grime, rot, rubbish, foam, wet sand, a tide line, driftwood or fallen fronds yourself: the dressing makes them from your plain walls, windows, timber, kerbs, roads, sand, water and trunks. Build the plain shapes, name them, and leave the old-and-used to step 2. Do build the things that make the place what was asked for (a boat, a water tank, a sign, a bench, the kind of building): those are shapes, and the dressing will age them.
- Do not add your own lights unless the judge asks for a light the look stage cannot give; then say so in the report.
- The painter (an image model repainting the plate for the flatter painted look) is not part of this loop. If it is wanted, the explainer-film plugin's `paint-and-cut` skill does it from the full-size picture.

## Traps

- A lamp whose glass has no glowing material gets no light: name its glass `lamp glass`. A fire's flame is `flame` or `fire`: it gets its own low, wide light and its own dial.
- `stone` means a built wall and gets laid up in ashlar; a boulder, rock or reef is `rock`.
- Shape the ground down under any water (a beach sloping into the sea, a bank down to a pond): the dressing finds the waterline from where the water covers the ground, and a beach cut level and straight at the water reads straight even with its swash line.
- Name a tree's living leaves `frond`, `foliage` or `canopy` (they get the weed material); `leaves` and `leaf` are fallen leaves. `palm`, `tree` and `trunk` give bark: build a tree's trunk and its leaves as two objects (`palm 3 trunk`, `palm 3 fronds`).
- The water's edges are dressed only where the camera sees ground meet water, nearer than about 70 m (things standing on the ground: about 40 to 60 m; trees: 45 m). The ground there needs a role (`sand`, `earth`, `rock`, `grass`...): unnamed ground gets no wet band, swash or tide line.
- Role words hide inside other names: `step`, `window` and `fire` take their role wherever they appear (`door step` is a pavement, `window frame` a dark window, `fire bucket` a flame). Words that name a thing (house, shop, harbour, beach, jetty, table, post, pond, tree) are read only after every material word, so give each object its material in its name: `tea house wall timber`, `harbour wall stone`, `jetty post timber`. An object named only `tea house` or `harbour` gets the thing's role (a stone wall, the sea). Plurals count (`floor flags` is a pavement). Read the dressing report, not your intention.
- A place laid along X instead of Y gets the stone picture smeared along its long walls; the procedural parts still work, but lay it along Y.
- The look stage replaces the sky. A sunset painted on a dome is gone; a `--keep-world` picture keeps it but loses the blue-hour sky light's colour.
- The first picture is usually too dark or too bright by one exposure step; that is what the measure is for, not a reason to change the lights.
- Measures inside every band do not mean the picture is right: balloon-shaped smoke and blob trees pass every band. Judge every round.
