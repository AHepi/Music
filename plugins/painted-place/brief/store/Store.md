# Store

What this file is: the store's catalogue. Ready **parts** (things to place), **tools** (code that makes a thing from a line, a point or an object you give it) and ready **materials** a builder may use. Nothing in the store places itself: where things go is yours to decide. Using the store is optional; anything you build yourself is just as welcome.

## How to use it, from your own Blender script

```python
import sys
sys.path.insert(0, FOLDER + '/store/tools')     # FOLDER is your copy of the brief folder
import bpy
import store

store.names('parts')                                   # what is on offer
store.names('materials')
tent = store.add_part('bell_tent', location=(5.0, 8.0, 0.0), turn=30, seed=4)   # a part where you say; returns its root
store.use_material(my_wall, 'SURFACE • old_stone_wall_02')                       # a material on your own object
import lay_walkway                                     # a tool: give it a line, it makes the thing along it
lay_walkway.lay([(0, 0, 0), (1.5, 4, 0), (2, 9, 0.2)], width=0.6, seed=2)
store.prepare_for_render(bpy.context.scene)            # once, just before drawing: real scales, ground heights, stone pictures fitted
```

Each tool also answers `python3 <tool>.py --help`. `python3 store.py --list` prints everything the store holds. The cards (pictures of every part and material) are in `cards/`; `cards/parts - all.jpg` and `cards/materials - all.jpg` show them all at once.

## Conventions

- Metres; Z up; ground at z = 0 under a part's foot.
- A part's origin is at its foot. Its front (a door, a muzzle, a lamp's face) faces **−Y**, which is what Blender's front view shows. Turn it with `turn` (degrees about Z, counter-clockwise seen from above).
- `add_part` puts every part in its own collection under one root (an empty); move or turn the root to move the whole part. Options a part accepts are on its row below; `seed` changes its small irregularities.
- Materials come in four kinds:
  - `SURFACE • <name>`: a photographed surface from the shelf (`surfaces/`), laid from three sides at the photograph's real size, already darkened into the look's range. Each has dials you may turn from a script: `mat.node_tree.nodes['Weather'].inputs['Wet' | 'Grime' | 'Mud height' | 'Mud color' | 'Edge wear' | 'Bump' | 'Darken']`, `mat.node_tree.nodes['Picture size'].inputs['Scale']` and `mat.node_tree.nodes['Normal picture'].inputs['Strength']` (the set's normal picture: 0 plain, 1 its full relief; the height picture's `Bump` adds a little over it). Turning a dial changes every object using that material; copy the material first (`mat.copy()`) to change one thing only.
  - `MADE • <name>`: materials made for the store: in code (old brass, old copper, glazed china, weathered boards with peeling paint, wet bark, puddle water, a wet-ground film, rusty nail heads, a rust stain, rail steel, smoke for a ribbon, a slate roof, stove ash, and ten stains the stain tool lays: soot, damp, rust, mud, coal dust, ash, and the heavy ones that show on fronts far down a street: heavy soot, far soot, leak, heavy damp), or from a shelf photograph with the same dials as a SURFACE (wet mud, army canvas, the walkway's boards).
  - `BRICK • <set>` and `ASHLAR • <set>`: photographed brick and stone-block walls whose every whole brick or block was found and listed (`materials/atlas/<set>.json`); `lay_wall` and `lay_stone` give each brick or block one of them through the UV map `pp_atlas`. On a mesh without that map they draw wrong.
  - the street's materials (setts, flags, glass, lamps, iron, moss, weeds, soot, smoke), under their own names.
- **Photographed stone and brick** (decision 80): `materials/Photographed.md` says which material is meant for setts, the road bed, flags, kerbs and dressings, ashlar, bricks, stone blocks and mortar; the store's road, kerb, wall and stone tools use them unless you name another (the street's old painted materials still answer by name). Each piece shows its own part of the photographs, turned, shifted and a little lighter or darker, when it carries the number `pp_piece` (0 to 1, on a mesh's faces or on the points that carry copies): the store's tools write it; give it to your own pieces and the `SURFACE` materials do the rest. Without it they draw as before. Each `SURFACE` material's node `Pieces` has the dials `Turn` and `Shade`.
- A surface material reads two numbers from each object: its real scale (`pp_scale`, so a photograph is never stretched by an unapplied scale) and the height of the ground under it (`pp_ground`, for the mud dial). `store.prepare_for_render(scene)`, called once just before drawing, records both for everything: the scale as it is then, and the ground as the foot of the part an object belongs to (for your own objects, the height you gave `use_material(obj, name, ground=z)`, or else the object's lowest point). The same call stretches the street's stone pictures over your place (the cobbles and stone tools need that). It moves nothing.
- Material names may be typed as the cards spell them: `store.material('SURFACE - brown_mud')` finds `SURFACE • brown_mud`; a name that matches nothing gets the nearest names suggested.
- A part given an option it does not have stops with the list of its options (a misspelt option never passes unnoticed).
- **Ground** comes in layers, each a tool, used in this order: `ground` makes the sheet the place stands on (its swells and dips, a road's camber along a line you give) and sets how wet it is, so water finds the low places first; `tracks` carves the ruts of a horse and cart, a car, a lorry or a tractor, a trodden path, footprints and potholes into that sheet's own height along lines you give, each mark as deep, lumpy, lipped, wet and worn as the ground under it and its age make it; then `grass` (turf, tussocks, tall weeds), `stones` (rocks, pebbles, grit) and `litter` (fallen leaves, twigs, trodden straw), each within an area you give, keeping out of the ruts and water the tracks left (`clear_under.clear` takes them away again from under anything you set down, a heap or a basket); last, `ground.fill(yard, ground.spots(yard))` puts water wherever it stands, the tracks' dips included, as flat sheets cut from the ground's own faces (`rain=` rings it with drops). The tools carve, wet and cover only the ground you give, along the lines and within the areas you give: where the lane, the ruts, the grass and the stones go is yours to decide. `ground.height_at(yard, x, y)` stands a part on the uneven ground. Two worked examples are in `examples/`: a horse and cart's track after rain (`ground - a cart track after rain.py`) and deep motor ruts in a wet field (`ground - deep ruts in a wet field.py`).
- **A paved street** is laid by its own tools: `lay_cobbles.lay_setts` (the road's setts) and `lay_cobbles.lay_flags` (the pavement's flags), then `lay_kerb` along the road's edge (its line is the kerb's top edge on the road side; `channel=` adds the dished gutter stones), then `joint_weeds` for grass and small weeds in the joints, kept out of the wheel lines you give. **A road that bends** (decision 80): `lay_cobbles.lay_setts_along(line, width)` lays the setts along a line or curve you give (the road's middle, whichever way it goes), in courses square to it that fan on the bends and climb with it, on a bed of its own; `lay_cobbles.road_edges(line, width)` gives the road's two edge lines for `lay_kerb`, and with `beyond=` and `rise=` the middle line of a pavement for `lay_cobbles.lay_flags_along(middle, width)`. On a road that climbs, `setts['top_height']` is the height at its first point only: ask `lay_cobbles.top_at(setts, x, y)` for a puddle's level anywhere. The setts are laid new and even unless you ask for wear: `shades` (each sett its own shade), `quarries` (setts from two quarries), `rocked` with `rocked_area` (setts sunk and tipped), `holes` with `hole_water` (setts gone, water in the holes), `patch` (a relaid patch of paler new setts with pitch in the joints), `grit` and `moss` (in the joints). What each option does and its default is on the tool's row below.
- **A wall on one plane** (decision 80): `lay_wall.lay(plane, kind='brick')` or `kind='stone'` fills one flat plane you give, whatever its size, standing any way or lying flat, with real bricks (stretcher, English, Flemish or header bond) or coursed stone blocks over a mortar back: whole courses from foot to top, the bond kept to both edges with half bricks and closers; a gable's slopes and holes in the plane are cut round. Each plane is one stretch: for a wall that turns, give it a plane per straight stretch.
- **A Japanese garden** (decision 82): parts for its things (`stone_lantern`, lit if asked; `water_basin` with its bamboo spout and ladle; `jar`; `clogs`; `hanging_lantern`; `wooden_bucket`; `bamboo_broom`) and its plants and bridge (`arched_bridge`, `maple_tree`, `garden_pine`, `fern`, `bamboo_clump`); tools for its buildings, used on a box in this order: `lay_tile_roof` (a curved tiled roof with turned-up corners, a front gable if asked), then `lay_veranda` (it reads the eave's height) and `lay_shoji` (paper screens in openings, glowing if asked); and for its ground: `lay_slab_path` (split slabs along a line, water in their hollows), `bamboo_fence` along a line, `water_lilies` within an area at the water's height, `far_hills` (misty ridges behind the place), `litter`'s `maple` leaves and `litter.heap`, and `puddle.make(..., mirror=0.6)` for water that must show the sky from above at dusk. Brought closer to the owner's picture (decision 86): `azalea` (a clipped mound in flower, or `flowering=False`), flowers on a hedge (`hedge.lay(..., flowers=1.0)`), `maple_tree(..., trunk=2.4)` to lift its crown off what stands behind it, `hanging_lantern(..., glow=0.25)` for a lantern near the eye, `stone_lantern(..., lit=True, light=30)` to light the ground round it in a wide place; for a low golden sun, `sky_and_haze.low_sun(scene, bearing=..., height=...)` (its sun light and a cloud deck drawn in the sky itself, gold near the sun and grey away from it), `sky_and_haze.mist_light(box, keep_out=[sun, fill])` so those lights leave the mist alone and a weak light of its own lights it, and `far_hills.lay(..., true_height=True, saddle=bearing, eye=eye)` for ridges that reach the height asked and dip where the sun sets (`far_hills.ridge_height(made, eye, bearing)` says how high the ridge stands there: put the sun 1 to 2 degrees under it).
- **A baked stand-in** (decision 87): `bake_to_pbr.bake(part_root, size=512, lit_through=0.3)` bakes a detailed part onto a 320-face dome as four PBR pictures (colour, relief, shine, height), with its height as real bumps; hide the part yourself. Tested on the azalea: from about 3 m on it reads as the built bush, and from 8 m at dusk it is the same bush with 256 or 512 pictures. It saves drawing time (8 against 13 seconds at 3 m, 10 against 21 at 1.8 m, 20 against 21 in a whole garden view), and file space only with small pictures: the built azalea is 0.92 MB because it is stored as points its leaves and flowers are copied onto; baked it is 0.36 MB with 256 pictures, about 1 MB with 512, 3.1 MB with 1024.
- **Building fronts**: `lay_stone` (photographed sandstone unless you give `shades=lay_stone.SHADES, trim_material=lay_stone.TRIM_MATERIAL` for the old painted stone; doors and windows in painted timber frames, decision 81, or `frames='stone'` for stone jambs, lintels and sills; `lit=[1, 3]` or `lit=0.3` for windows that glow, each still needing a small warm light outside it) stands its blocks out in front of the wall box it is given, their faces up to about 0.24 m out (it records its relief on the box as `stone_front`). The tools that hang things on a front measure from that face: `lay_eaves` puts the slate roof on with its overhang, eaves boards, rafter ends and gutters (its gutter outlets come back for the downpipes); `downpipe` hangs a pipe from a gutter outlet or a top point down to the ground, with its leak stains if asked; `stain.on_wall(..., offset='front')` lays a stain just in front of the blocks. `weathering.stone` and `weathering.peel` give back weathered copies of a material (rain streaks and soot blotches on stone, paint peeling to grey timber): give the copies to the things you choose, and the store's own material stays as it was.

## Traps

- The surface materials are laid at the photograph's real size: an object scaled up after `use_material` shows a stretched photograph until `store.prepare_for_render` runs. Call it once, last, just before drawing.
- Setts are laid with their tops at the road's own height (recorded as `setts['top_height']`) and the road itself lowered 12.5 cm under them as the bed: stand things at that height, keep any other ground at least 13 cm below it, and for a puddle among setts give `puddle.make(..., level=setts['top_height'] + 0.002)` (rays cannot hit the setts, which are drawn as copies; a puddle given the cobbled road as ground stops with this advice).
- A part's front is −Y. A part turned with `turn=180` faces +Y.
- The puddle materials need the numbers the puddle tool writes on its meshes ('shallow', 'dry'); on your own mesh without them the water is deep all over and the film wet all over.
- The street's stone materials (`Hand-painted sandstone • shade NN`, `STONE • worn honey-grey architectural dressings`, `PAVEMENT • damp sandstone slabs`) project one big picture over the whole place: they are for walls, pavements and the stone tool, not for a single small thing (on a headstone the courses squeeze into stripes). For a small stone thing use `SURFACE • old_stone_wall_02`, `SURFACE • sandstone_cracks`, or the headstone's own stone.
- A stain or wear factor built from a noise's Fac sits between about 0.4 and 0.6, and the AgX view flattens the darks: push a stain much harder than it looks in the nodes, and judge it in a drawn picture, never by its numbers.
- The card pictures are drawn under one amber light and a blue fill, brighter than a night scene: a material looks darker in your place. Volumes (chimney smoke, haze) draw nothing on a card.
- The pictures are found on the shelf by file name. Copy the whole brief folder, never the store alone, or the surface materials draw pink.
- A `SURFACE` material on Geometry Nodes copies lays a whole picture squeezed onto each copy, because it reads the copy's own small shape: `lay_cobbles` gives its setts and flags their own copies of the material (`<material> • stone sized`) that show the pictures at their real size. For copies of your own, use real meshes or do the same.
- Give a bend's points evenly spaced (about 2 to 3 m apart): points spaced unevenly round a circle make the smoothed curve bend tighter than the circle, and the road tools may refuse it.
- A bend has a tightest radius: a road's at least 1.33 times half its width (3.4 m for a 5 m road), a pavement's at least its own width; anything tighter is refused with the radius it needs. A pavement round the inside of a road's bend is on a tighter line than the road.
- `lay_wall` lays its bricks on the side its plane faces: if they come out behind it, give `flip=True`. Where two planes meet at a corner the bricks do not wrap round it.
- Lit paper and lit lanterns glow but light little round them: `lay_shoji` gives back the places for a small warm light outside each lit screen; many lit screens side by side draw far too bright.
- `far_hills` stand 120 m and more away: Blender's camera draws nothing past 100 m until `camera.data.clip_end` is raised. `far_hills.lay` gives back the far limit they need as `far_limit`. Without `true_height=True` each ridge stands lower than asked (0.3 to 0.9 of the height, 0.7 on average), and lower still across a narrow view.
- `far_hills` cast no shadow, so a sun just behind a ridge still lights the place and lays a blinding strip of light on wet ground before the eye. Letting the hills cast shadow is true to life but turns the place cool (warm 11% in the helper's test): the warm must then come from lamps.
- `sky_and_haze.low_sun` draws the sun's centre white at the builders' usual finishing brightness, and its clouds stay put if the camera travels tens of metres.
- The `azalea` goes nearly black at dusk, its flowers a dim dusky pink, unless a lantern stands within a metre or two of it; out of flower it shows only by its outline against something paler. A hedge in flower reads as a loose flowering hedge, not a clipped azalea row: for a clipped row use the azalea with several `mounds`.
- A `hanging_lantern` near the eye fills the warm-light measure by itself at its full glow: `glow=0.25` draws it as warm paper with its lattice showing.
- A baked stand-in from `bake_to_pbr` shows its flowers flat and soft closer than about 3 m (at 1.8 m), and its outline is smoother than the part's; without its bumps it reads as a shiny painted dome. It casts no leaf shadows of its own and does not follow later changes to the part: bake again after changing it. Like the built azalea, it needs a lantern within a metre or two at dusk.
- Leaves and petals made of one face that faces into a bush bake as dark dents in a normal picture (a dark swirl on the first baked azalea): `bake_to_pbr` turns them outwards first; a bake of your own must do the same.
- `card.py material all` redraws the `BRICK` and `ASHLAR` cards as a plain sphere and cube; draw them again with `python3 materials/src/photographed_cards.py`.
