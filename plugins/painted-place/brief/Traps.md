# Traps

What this file is: the faults builders working from this brief have run into so far (and the faults the owner saw in their pictures), so the next one loses no time on them. Nothing here says how to build; it says what has gone wrong.

- **A lamp whose glass or casing surrounds its light gives no light.** Every builder's first lamps lit nothing. Put the light outside the glass, or let the glass pass light.
- **Haze that looks right from inside the scene is far too thick in the picture.** First pictures came out as brown or milky fog. Start thin; thin it with height; make it bluer than you think.
- **Puddles turn into black holes or sheets of pale paper.** Causes found: a sky too bright in the reflections; puddle shapes facing down; water as one big flood. A long puddle that mirrors one light reads better than many small ones.
- **A puddle drawn as a flat glossy disc reads as a cutout mirror** (the owner's words for it). A real puddle has a ragged edge, a dark wet rim of ground round it, mud showing through where it is shallow, and a faint ripple that breaks the reflection.
- **Pale ground reads as snow, plaster or hammered tin.** Dusk ground is dark, damp and brown-grey; judge it next to the brightest light it will have.
- **Smoke made of blobs reads as balloons; smoke painted on afterwards is not lit by the lamps.** A soft ribbon facing the camera, with see-through edges, has worked. If you cannot make it read, leave it out.
- **Colour gradients with stops outside 0 to 1 silently go flat** in Blender's colour ramps; rescale the input first.
- **A volume's density measured from the world's centre comes out empty** when the box stands elsewhere; measure from the object's own coordinates.
- **The tall 9:16 frame is narrow near the camera:** about 3 m wide at 9 m with a 32 mm lens. A building placed near the camera fills a third of the picture; things 10 m to the side are out of frame. Check the frame before dressing.
- **Light makes visible shafts only where it comes through a small gap** (a few clear panes, a door ajar). A whole wall of lit glass makes a glow, not shafts.
- **Lit glass is counted by its area, not by how many windows glow.** Big panes lit all over draw far too bright whatever the exposure; light one or two and leave the rest dim or dark.
- **A dark hero against a dark background disappears** (a black urn, a gun barrel). Give it a light of its own or a lighter ground behind it.
- **A thing nobody can name at the final size is noise.** The owner could not tell what the small objects on a walkway were. Make each thing big enough and lit enough to be named, or take it out; litter on a path is the usual case.
- **A building drawn as a box with a plank picture on it reads as cardboard.** It needs eaves with the roof overhanging and its shadow under it, boards or stones in relief, a door, a chimney or stove pipe, and corner boards or quoins.
- **Words, people and hands break the look.** House numbers, signs with letters, figures: none.
- **The period is fixed by the look page unless the request says otherwise:** "a suburban townhouse" came out as a Victorian terrace. If another era is wanted, it must be in the request.
- **The clock, not a fixed number of rounds, decides when to stop.** Measure every picture, look at every picture at full size, and keep fixing while time allows; a builder who stopped after one fix pass with an hour left handed in a picture it knew was broken.
- **Inside every band is a floor, not the finish.** The measure says the colour and light are not wrong; it says nothing about detail. Three builders stopped 30 to 49 minutes early because their picture measured well, and each handed in a place with clean, plain shapes left in it.
- **"The remaining faults are small and more changes are risky" is not the end either.** A builder with the store stopped 63 minutes early for that reason, with plain bare ground, few small things and thin haze left in its picture. Keep a copy of the script that drew the last good picture, then change one thing at a time: a change that goes wrong costs one picture, not the place.
- **A haze box that ends inside the sky draws a hard step across it.** Thin the haze with height so it is gone before the box's top (a builder's sky showed a hard amber band until it did).
- **The shelf's `muddy_tracks` reads orange and crinkled like foil under amber light** on a wide ground; `mud_forest` with its Wet dial up, or the store's `MADE • wet mud`, reads as mud.
- **A glowing pane lights nothing round it by itself.** Give each lit window or door left ajar a small warm light of its own just outside the glass, or behind an open gap (a goods yard builder used about 40 W each); the store's lit glass is an opaque glow and blocks a light set behind it. Without one the glass glows while the ground in front of it stays dark.
- **With the `bpy` module, `import bpy` must come before `import bmesh`.**
- **Tracks drawn as a picture on flat ground look painted under a low lamp:** nothing casts a shadow, nothing holds water, and a stone sits across the groove. Ruts, footprints and potholes need real depth in the ground; the store's tracks tool carves them.
- **Water that reaches past the ground's edge draws a dark rectangle in the distance.** Make the ground bigger than the frame shows, or leave out the water at its edge.
- **A light inside a closed building lights nothing you can see.** A barn builder's cow-house lamp sat inside solid walls; a lantern by the door, or a building left hollow with its door open, lights the yard.
- **Lamps seen through an open door draw as glowing balls.** Hide those lights from the eye and let them light the hay, the walls and the cart instead.
- **Haze inside a lit barn or room turns everything in it into flat glowing fog.** Keep the haze outside the building; light the inside from one side so its things cast shadows.
- **Straw or hay strands spread out from one middle point read as stars.** Laid side by side in clumps, pressed flat and darker where trodden, they read as straw.
- **Asking the ground for its height once per small thing is slow:** once per straw strand took 90 seconds for one yard. Ask once per clump.
- **Anything behind a wall taller than about 1.2 m is hidden from eye height.** A gate swung into the field behind a dry-stone wall disappeared; swung into the yard, it shows.
- **Root crops drawn round, pale and pink read as peaches.** Mangolds are long and tapering, dull orange to red, with soil caked on their tails.
- **A sack seen small reads as a boulder.** Give it a tied neck and folds, or leave it out.
- **Stains, damp runs and rust streaks on a building 19 m away do not show** at 540 x 960. Make them big and strong, or put them on things near the eye.
- **A lit lamp near the eye steals the picture from the hero.** A town street builder left the nearest lamp unlit (the lamplighter has not reached it yet) and lit the setts from a lamp out of frame.
- **A light in front of a lit shop window draws as a white slab; a lamp post next to thin gutter water draws as a black cut-out in it.** Hide the light from the eye; move the water or the post.
- **Small puddles near the eye mirror the dark fronts, not the sky, and read as black blots.** Near the eye a puddle must lie where it mirrors a light, or go.
- **Where a taller building rises above a lower neighbour, its side wall shows above the roof as a plain slab.** Give that wall stone, a chimney stack or a gable too.
- **Stains on a relief stone front must stand out past the stones** (the store's stone blocks stand about 0.23 m out); stains laid close to the wall are cut off behind the blocks. The stain tool's `offset='front'` sets them out past the store's blocks.
- **On the side fronts of a street, flat things are seen edge-on.** What stands out from a wall (signs on brackets, pots on sills, downpipes) reads there; flat things (stains, blinds) read on the fronts that face the eye, and must be broad on the side ones.
- **Measure the ground's height before anything is set down on it:** a ray from above finds the top of whatever already stands there (a street builder's dirt floated 40 cm up on a stack of setts until it did).
- **The water of a puddle nearer the eye than a lamp's reflection mirrors the dark sky above the lamp** and reads as a black streak: put the reflection at the puddle's near end. A puddle's clear rim draws a pale outline round the water under a lamp; a narrower rim (`shallow_band=0.04`) keeps it clean.
- **A puddle under an eave mirrors the eave and reads as black;** water seen from above mirrors only a little of a dusk sky, so its mirror has to be made stronger to read.
- **Fallen leaves with narrow lobes read as stars,** and shiny leaves under a blue dusk sky turn pink: broad toothed lobes, little shine.
- **Small things built from boxes read as boxes** (clogs, buckets); things smaller than about 3 cm cannot be seen at 540 x 960. A dark thing in a big shadow reads with pale materials or bright metal on it, without a light of its own.
- **One thing standing in front of another cancels both** (a broom before a bucket): set them apart.
- **A stop command that names a process also stops the shell running it** (`pkill -f render_final` matches its own command line): match with a bracket, `pkill -f "[r]ender_final"`. Both garden builders lost a step this way.
- **Blender's camera stops seeing at 100 m by default:** far mountains vanish until its far limit is raised.
- **A pale thing on pale wet flags vanishes** (a newspaper on buff sandstone): put it where the ground is dark, in the gutter or on the setts.
- **Two faces lying in exactly the same plane draw as black squares** (where two timbers cross, where a frame meets its panel, where a flat thing's top lies exactly on the road). Make one of them a few millimetres shallower, or lift it 1 mm.
- **Worn iron made shiny like a mirror reads dark, not bright:** at night it mirrors the dark sky. Make the worn tops lighter and only half metal, the rust in the hollows darker.
- **By day an open doorway reads as a black hole.** The sky's light through a door is too little to show the inside; a soft cool light just inside the door, facing in and hidden from the eye, shows it dimmer than the sunlit outside. Lanterns are unlit by day.
