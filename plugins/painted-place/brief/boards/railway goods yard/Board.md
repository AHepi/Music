# Reference board: a railway goods yard

What this folder is: real photographs of railway yards, freight sheds and their things from 1900 to 1943, gathered for the request "a railway goods yard" (Library of Congress; Detroit Publishing Company, Lewis Hine's National Child Labor Committee photographs, the D.C. Street Survey, Farm Security Administration / Office of War Information, Historic American Buildings Survey; all public domain or with no known restrictions; `sources.json` has each picture's source). They show what the place is made of and how it was used. They do not show the look (the look is in `reference/`); the period's photographs are daylight and grey.

Not found as public-domain photographs, so from memory or the builder's own knowledge: a yard crane close up; a water tower close up (03 has a coaling shed only); setts or cobbles between the rails; horse carts at a British shed; the inside of a shed office; British wagon types (private-owner open wagons, vans with lime-washed roofs).

## Traps

- These are mostly American yards (and 07, 08, 11 are from 1942-43, after the period asked for); the request is British. Use them for materials, wear and use, not for wagon shapes, lettering or signal styles.
- 04, 05 and 06 are sepia prints, 07 and 08 are press negatives with film edges; take the ground, the coal and the platforms from them, never the tone or the black borders.
- 09 and 10 were photographed long after their working years; their patched roofs and weeds are the end of a yard's life, not its busy years.
- The pictures are photographs of daylight; everything in them is lighter and greyer than the look wants.
