# Reference board: a town street

What this folder is: real photographs of British town streets and closes, with two of a tenement street in New York, gathered for the request to do "the town street" (Library of Congress: stereograph cards, the Detroit Publishing Company, and prints of Edinburgh closes; all with no known restrictions; `sources.json` has each picture's source). They show what a town street of about 1900 to 1920 is made of and how it is used: tenements and shops, setts and kerbs, lamps, awnings, barrows. They do not show the look (the look is in `reference/`, where "Rain on the Old Curve" is a town street in the look); the period's photographs are daylight and grey. The years in the file names are the year of the photograph.

Not found as public-domain photographs, so from memory or the builder's own knowledge: a town street in rain at dusk, its setts shining with the lamps (the reference pictures show it); a northern mill town's terraced back street with yard walls, back gates and outside privies; a close view of setts with a cast-iron drain grate and a kerbstone worn by cart wheels; a lamplighter's gas lamp close to; tenement backs with wash houses and drying lines.

## Traps

- Words on shop boards, signs and posters belong to the photographs; the look allows no words in a picture, so boards stay blank or too far off to read.
- 05 to 08 are city centre streets at their busiest; a town street of tenements is plainer and quieter: fewer shops, no trams.
- 09 and 10 are American: iron fire escapes on the fronts are not British; take the barrows, awnings, crates and setts from them, not the buildings.
- The stereograph frames (03 to 08 and 10) are cropped from cards; a little of a card's edge may show at a corner.
- The pictures are photographs of daylight; everything in them is lighter and greyer than the look wants.
