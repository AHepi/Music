# Reference board: a Japanese garden

What this folder is: real photographs of Japanese gardens and of the timber buildings beside them, gathered for the request "a Japanese garden after rain beside a traditional timber inn, in a mountain valley" (Library of Congress: stereograph cards published from 1896 to 1904; all with no known restrictions on publication; `sources.json` has each picture's source). They show what such a garden and its buildings are made of, how they are built, and how they have aged and been used: ponds and stepping stones, rocks and banks, stone lanterns and a stone water basin, wooden bridges, lotus, bamboo and bamboo fences, pruned pines, timber verandas, tiled and thatched roofs, paper lanterns and paper screens. They do not show the look (the look is in `reference/`); the period's photographs are daylight and grey, and none was taken in rain. The years in the file names are the year the card was published.

## Traps

- Words on the inn's curtains, lanterns and signs belong to the photographs; the look allows no words in a picture, so curtains and lanterns stay blank or too far off to read.
- 05 is a famous temple pavilion and 06 the courtyard of a great temple; a garden beside an inn is smaller and plainer: take from them how roofs, railings, posts over water and stone lanterns are built, not their size or the rows of lanterns.
- 11's roof is thatch; the inn in the request has curved tiled roofs (06 and 10 show tiles): take 11's paper lanterns, veranda and timber, not its thatch.
- 10's sliding windows are glass; paper screens (12) are white paper on a wooden lattice, with light coming through them.
- 01, 02 and 04 are large public gardens, and the grassy hill in 01 is a model of Mount Fuji; a garden beside an inn is smaller and closer: take their stepping stones, banks, rocks and water, not their open lawns.
- The cards are printed in brown and reddish tones (04 most of all); that colour belongs to the prints, not to the places.
- Every photograph is dry and in daylight; after rain, stone and timber go darker, moss greener, and stepping stones, paths and roofs shine, and none of that is in them.
- Each picture is one frame cropped from a stereograph card, only about 400 pixels across: look in them for shapes, how things are built and where wear and moss sit, not for fine texture.
