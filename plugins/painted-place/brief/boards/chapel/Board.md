# Reference board: a chapel

What this folder is: real photographs of small chapels and meeting houses, outside and inside, gathered for the request "a chapel" (Library of Congress; Farm Security Administration / Office of War Information, the Carnegie Survey of the Architecture of the South, Historic American Buildings Survey; all public domain or with no known restrictions; `sources.json` has each picture's source). They show what the place is made of and how it was used. They do not show the look (the look is in `reference/`); the period's photographs are daylight and grey. The years in the file names are the year of the photograph where the source gives it, and "built" with the year of building where it does not (the Historic American Buildings Survey photographs were taken at some time after 1933).

## Traps

- All of these are American (the Library of Congress holds almost no public-domain photographs of British chapels); the request is a British Nonconformist one. Use them for materials, building and wear, not for details of style: the box pews and tall canopied pulpit in 10 and 12 are older Anglican fittings, not a plain chapel's.
- Several are very old buildings (1775 to 1838); the chapel asked for is of 1850 to 1940, so make its stone or brick newer and its glass plainer.
- 03 and 08 and 09 are colour transparencies taken in recent decades; take the wood and glass from them, not the colour balance.
- The pictures are photographs of daylight; everything in them is lighter and greyer than the look wants.
